# Category and competitive positioning

Use when the audience cannot quickly place the offer, the copy resembles every competitor, or the business is competing in the wrong mental category.

## Operating principles

- Positioning is the audience's shorthand for what the offer is, who it is for, and why it matters relative to known choices.
- Begin from the audience's existing frame of reference; explain novelty through familiar anchors.
- Choose a category that makes the offer's strongest advantages relevant.
- Compete for a specific place in the audience's mind rather than a comprehensive list of virtues.
- Name realistic alternatives, including manual work, internal builds, general-purpose tools, incumbents, and doing nothing.
- Favor a clear, defensible focus over a broad claim of superiority.

## Positioning test

Complete: “For `[best-fit audience]` who `[high-stakes situation]`, `[offer]` is a `[category/frame]` that `[distinct value]`. Unlike `[real alternative]`, it `[differentiated mechanism or capability]`, supported by `[proof]`.”

Treat this as a thinking scaffold, not mandatory published copy.

## Misuse guards

Do not invent a category merely to sound original. Do not claim leadership without evidence. Do not define differentiation only with adjectives such as seamless, innovative, or all-in-one.
