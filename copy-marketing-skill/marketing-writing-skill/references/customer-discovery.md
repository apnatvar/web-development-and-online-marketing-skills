# Customer discovery

Use when claims about pain, demand, workflow, willingness to switch, or buying behavior rest on guesses.

## Operating principles

- Prefer specific past behavior over opinions about hypothetical futures.
- Ask about the customer's life and workflow before describing the solution.
- Seek facts, constraints, workarounds, spend, frequency, and consequences.
- Treat praise and feature suggestions as weak evidence unless tied to commitment or behavior.
- Separate a politely acknowledged problem from an urgent, costly problem.
- Do not lead the witness with the answer the business hopes to hear.

## Practical moves

Convert “Customers love speed” into questions or evidence requests: What took longest last time? What did delay cost? What workaround was used? Who approved a purchase? Then translate verified patterns into copy using the customer's natural level of specificity.

When no research exists, write provisional language and label the assumption. Recommend the smallest useful validation: five recent-event interviews, support-ticket review, sales-call analysis, search-query review, or behavioral data.

## Misuse guards

Do not present invented interview quotes as customer language. Do not treat survey intent as purchase proof. Do not conduct discovery inside public-facing copy.
