# Market awareness and desire

Use when the copy explains too much or too little, the audience's familiarity varies, or the market is saturated with similar promises.

## Awareness ladder

| State | Audience knows | Lead with |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Neither problem nor offer | A recognizable situation, tension, or insight; avoid a hard sell |
| Problem-aware | The pain, not the best solution | Consequence, diagnosis, and desired change |
| Solution-aware | A class of solutions | Mechanism, approach, and decision criteria |
| Product-aware | The offer, not confidence | Differentiation, proof, objections, and fit |
| Most aware | The offer and value | Specific proposal, timing, or direct CTA |

Match sophistication too. In a mature market, a louder version of the standard promise is weak; explain a credible mechanism, narrow the audience, demonstrate proof, or reframe the problem.

## Desire principles

Channel an existing human or business desire; do not pretend copy creates demand from nothing. Connect product capabilities to outcomes through a believable causal chain.

## Misuse guards

Do not label awareness by demographic stereotype. Do not use escalating promises to compensate for weak evidence. Do not mistake familiarity with readiness.
