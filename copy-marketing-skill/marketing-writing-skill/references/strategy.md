# Strategic diagnosis and focused action

Use when the brief contains many goals, a slogan instead of a strategy, or tactics that do not address the central obstacle.

## Strategy kernel

- **Diagnosis:** Explain the pivotal challenge simply enough to guide choices.
- **Guiding approach:** Define how the business will address that challenge and what it will not try to do.
- **Coherent actions:** Align message, proof, offer, channel, and CTA with the approach.

Find leverage: a concentrated insight, capability, audience segment, timing advantage, or constraint that makes focused action more effective. Treat tradeoffs as part of strategy.

## Practical moves

Replace “increase awareness and conversions across all channels” with a diagnosis such as “best-fit buyers mistake the product for a general reporting tool.” Choose a guiding approach—own decision-ready incident reporting for regulated teams—and align assets around that contrast.

## Misuse guards

Do not confuse ambition, values, targets, or a task list with strategy. Do not manufacture a dramatic enemy. Do not add channels that dilute the guiding approach.

Framework-level inspiration: *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* by Richard Rumelt. All guidance here is paraphrased and adapted.
