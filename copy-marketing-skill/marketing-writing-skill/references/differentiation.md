# Differentiation and best-fit relevance

Use when the product is described as a feature bundle, its value depends on context, or the best customer and competitive set are unclear.

## Operating principles

Work backward through this chain:

1. Identify real alternatives.
2. Isolate capabilities those alternatives lack or execute differently.
3. Translate capabilities into outcomes that matter.
4. Identify customers who care most about those outcomes.
5. Choose a market frame that makes the value obvious.
6. Add trends only when they strengthen urgency or relevance.

Distinguish a feature, capability, benefit, business outcome, and proof. “Automated tagging” is a feature; “find the correct record without manual sorting” is a benefit; “shorter resolution time” is an outcome that still needs evidence.

## Practical moves

Build a comparison around meaningful decision criteria, not a vanity feature grid. State who should not choose the offer when that sharpens trust and fit.

## Misuse guards

Do not assume uniqueness means value. Do not choose a category solely because it is large. Do not position against a caricatured competitor.
