# Motivation, persuasion, and choice

Use when copy must reduce hesitation, explain a consequential choice, or motivate action without pressure. These models generate hypotheses; they do not diagnose a reader or guarantee behaviour.

## Framework status

- **Research-grounded models/effects:** Self-Determination Theory, Expectancy Theory, regulatory focus, COM-B, Elaboration Likelihood Model, Prospect Theory, framing, anchoring, defaults, present bias, social norms, and choice overload. Effects vary by context and study design.
- **Older or simplified need models:** Maslow and Herzberg can prompt questions but should not be treated as universal hierarchies or complete motivation theories.
- **Practitioner models:** Fogg B=MAP and applications of Cialdini's principles are useful design heuristics; test them ethically.

## Selection map

| Diagnostic signal | Use | Copy decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader feels controlled, incapable, or isolated | Self-Determination Theory | preserve choice, make the step achievable, show relevant support |
| Outcome matters but action feels futile | Expectancy Theory | clarify outcome value, plausible path, and link between action and result |
| Goal is growth or protection | Regulatory focus | promotion framing for aspiration; prevention framing for responsibility and safety |
| Action fails despite interest | COM-B or Fogg B=MAP | identify capability/ability, opportunity, motivation, or prompt barrier |
| Decision is high involvement | Elaboration Likelihood Model | provide substantive argument, comparison, proof, and limitations |
| Price or option presentation shapes perception | behavioural-economics audit | disclose reference point, total cost, options, defaults, and tradeoffs |
| Social or authority cues are proposed | Cialdini audit | verify representativeness, expertise, affiliation, limits, and consent |

## Motivation lenses

### Self-Determination Theory

**Autonomy:** offer meaningful choices and neutral ways to decline. **Competence:** make requirements, effort, and next steps understandable. **Relatedness:** use relevant community or support without implying that non-users do not belong.

Avoid commands, shame, guilt, or copy that diminishes the reader's current coping strategy.

### Expectancy and regulatory focus

Expectancy-based copy must connect three things: the reader values the outcome, the action can plausibly produce it, and the reader can perform the action. If any link is weak, repair the offer or explanation rather than intensifying the CTA.

Promotion-focused language emphasizes advancement and gains; prevention-focused language emphasizes responsibility and avoiding credible harm. Match the real context. Never manufacture danger or certainty.

### Maslow and Herzberg

Use Maslow only as a loose prompt to consider physiological security, belonging, esteem, and growth needs. Needs can coexist and their order varies. Do not infer a person's “level.”

Use Herzberg's distinction as a prompt: hygiene factors can remove dissatisfaction, while motivators can create positive value. In customer copy, translate this cautiously into basic expectations versus meaningful benefits; the original theory concerns work motivation and does not automatically predict purchase behaviour.

## Behaviour diagnosis

**COM-B — research-grounded behaviour-change model:** ask whether the reader has capability, opportunity, and motivation. A copy change can clarify or prompt, but cannot create time, access, authority, money, or product usability.

**Fogg B=MAP — practitioner model:** behaviour requires motivation, ability, and a prompt to coincide. Reduce legitimate effort, make the action specific, and prompt at an appropriate moment. Do not use repeated prompts to override refusal.

## Persuasion depth

**Elaboration Likelihood Model — research-grounded:** high-consequence or high-interest choices require stronger reasoning and evidence. Under low attention, improve relevance and comprehension; credible cues may guide attention but must not replace material facts.

**Cialdini principles — research-informed applications:** reciprocity, commitment/consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, and unity may suggest tests. Use only genuine evidence, limits, expertise, and affiliations. A testimonial sample must not imply typical outcomes without support.

## Behavioural-economics audit

Check:

- **reference dependence and anchoring:** is the comparison baseline relevant and disclosed?
- **loss aversion and framing:** are gains and losses accurate, proportionate, and presented with material context?
- **defaults and status quo:** is the default beneficial, visible, and easy to change?
- **present bias:** are immediate effort and delayed benefit both clear?
- **choice overload:** can options be grouped or compared without hiding alternatives?
- **social proof and norms:** is the evidence genuine, representative, and current?
- **scarcity:** is the limit real, specific, and verifiable?

Do not use decoys, hidden opt-ins, drip pricing, confirmshaming, fake countdowns, obstructive cancellation, misleading popularity, or fear without an effective and proportionate action.

## Output

Record privately:

- barrier or choice risk;
- model selected and evidence available;
- sentence, proof, order, option, or CTA changed;
- ethical check and material disclosure;
- validation signal.

If the model changes nothing, omit it.
