# Market and customer fit

Use when a brief jumps from product features to copy without deciding whom to serve, what progress matters, or whether the offer and delivery support the promise.

## Framework status

STP, Jobs to Be Done, the Value Proposition Canvas, marketing-mix models, and Kano are professional methods. They organise evidence; they do not prove demand. Validate assumptions with observed behaviour, interviews, sales/support records, search behaviour, or experiments.

## Selection map

| Decision | Framework | Inputs | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Which audience should this message serve? | STP | meaningful segments, needs, behaviour, buying role, reachability, business fit | target segment, exclusions, positioning requirement |
| What progress is the customer seeking? | Jobs to Be Done | situation, trigger, current workaround, desired progress, consequences, switching barriers | job statement and decision criteria |
| Which value should the offer communicate? | Value Proposition Canvas | verified jobs, pains, gains, capabilities, proof | capability-to-outcome chain and unsupported gaps |
| Is copy compensating for an offer problem? | 4Ps/7Ps plus 4Cs or SAVE | offer, price, access, service process, people, evidence, total customer cost | marketing-mix mismatch and correction |
| Which benefit is table stakes or differentiating? | Kano plus points of parity/difference | customer expectations and comparative evidence | basic expectation, performance benefit, possible delighter |

## STP

Segment by differences that change the decision: situation, need, desired outcome, behaviour, constraints, maturity, buying role, or alternative. Select a target because the offer has credible relevance and can reach it. Position relative to the alternative that segment actually uses.

Do not treat a demographic label as a need. Separate users, buyers, approvers, blockers, and influencers when their criteria differ.

## Jobs to Be Done

Draft the job as:

> When `[situation or trigger]`, I want to `[progress]`, so I can `[desired outcome]`.

Test it against a recent event: what triggered action, what was tried, what did the workaround cost, what created anxiety, who influenced the decision, and what prevented switching? Turn the validated job into situational relevance, not invented customer quotations.

## Value Proposition Canvas

Prioritise verified jobs, pains, and gains. Map each proposed benefit through a causal chain:

`capability → changed task or experience → customer outcome → evidence`

If a link is missing, weaken the claim, request proof, or omit it. Do not list every feature against every pain.

## Marketing-mix audit

- **4Ps:** Product, Price, Place, Promotion.
- **7Ps for services:** add People, Process, Physical Evidence.
- **4Cs:** Customer, Cost, Convenience, Communication.
- **SAVE:** Solution, Access, Value, Education.

Use a seller-side model and a customer-side model when promotion may be hiding weak fit. Examples include a low-friction claim paired with difficult onboarding, premium positioning without proof, a trial that requires an unexpected commitment, or service promises unsupported by process and people.

Copy cannot repair the mismatch. Escalate it, revise the offer if authorized, or state the limitation honestly.

## Kano and positioning

Treat basic expectations as requirements, not headline differentiators. Performance needs can support comparative value when evidence exists. Delighters are temporary and context-specific; do not assume novelty equals value.

Establish category points of parity before points of difference. Attach a reason to believe to every material difference. Use [category and competitive positioning](positioning.md) for the final positioning scaffold.

## Misuse guards

- Do not fabricate research, demand, pain, customer language, market size, or competitive weakness.
- Do not target protected or vulnerable groups with exploitative claims.
- Do not claim that a framework score predicts purchase.
- Do not force every customer into one linear funnel.
- Do not use “customer-centric” language while hiding price, effort, risk, or access constraints.
