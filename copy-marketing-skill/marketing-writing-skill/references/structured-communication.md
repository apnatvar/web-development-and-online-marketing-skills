# Structured communication

Use when the conclusion is buried, supporting points overlap, or long-form copy reads as a stream of facts.

## Operating principles

- Lead with the governing answer or recommendation when the audience is ready for it.
- Group supporting ideas that answer the same question.
- Make every group collectively exhaustive enough for the decision and avoid avoidable overlap.
- Order points by logic: importance, chronology, process, structure, or comparison.
- Use a situation-complication-question-answer sequence when the audience needs the problem framed before the answer.
- Ensure evidence supports the claim immediately above it.

## Practical moves

Write the one-sentence conclusion first. Ask what three to five questions a skeptical reader would ask next. Turn the answers into sections. Remove any section that does not support the conclusion or required action.

## Misuse guards

Do not create artificial groups of three. Do not front-load a conclusion the audience lacks context to interpret. Do not confuse hierarchy with repetitive summaries.

Framework-level inspiration: *The Pyramid Principle* by Barbara Minto. All guidance here is paraphrased and adapted.
