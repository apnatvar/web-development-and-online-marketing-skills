# Copy and content structures

Use when the central idea and evidence are sound but the asset lacks a useful order. These are practitioner heuristics, not persuasion laws. Select a structure because it fits the reader's state, evidence, channel, and action—not because its acronym is familiar.

## Selection map

| Need | Structure | Required inputs | Output shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General promotional sequence | AIDA or ACCA | relevant opening, interest/comprehension, credible desire/conviction, action | attention → understanding/value → proof → CTA |
| Known problem with a supported solution | PAS | real problem, proportionate consequence, mechanism | problem → consequence → solution |
| Make change tangible | Before–After–Bridge | verified current state, plausible future state, path | before → after → bridge |
| Translate a capability into relevance | FAB or feature–benefit–proof | feature, changed task, outcome, evidence | capability → advantage → benefit → proof |
| Trust matters more than excitement | problem–solution–proof–CTA or promise–proof–plan | supported problem, solution, evidence, next step | explicit argument with proof before action |
| Explain analysis or a recommendation | SCQA or Pyramid Principle | context, tension, question, supported answer | answer first with grouped support, or situation → complication → answer |
| Educational social post | Hook–Value–CTA, Problem–Mistake–Fix, Myth–Reality–Evidence | relevant hook, useful insight, support, proportionate action | concise learning sequence |
| Short narrative or script | Context–Conflict–Resolution, ABT, or story spine | real context, tension, resolution, takeaway | compressed narrative without invented drama |
| Plan an editorial portfolio | Hero–Hub–Help or content pillars | campaign moments, recurring expertise, common questions | balanced programme rather than one post |
| Match content to readiness | See–Think–Do–Care or awareness ladder | reader knowledge, intent, relationship, risk | appropriate detail, proof, and CTA |

## Direct-response structures

**AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.** Use when a reader needs a clear progression. Attention must be relevant, not sensational. Desire must follow from supported value.

**ACCA — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action.** Prefer for unfamiliar or consequential offers where understanding and proof matter more than emotional escalation.

**PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution.** Replace “agitate” with a proportionate explanation of consequence. Never intensify fear, shame, or insecurity beyond the evidence.

**Before–After–Bridge.** Make the before and after specific and plausible. Do not imply guaranteed transformation.

**FAB — Feature, Advantage, Benefit.** Show the causal chain. Add proof for substantive benefits and omit advantages that do not matter to this audience.

**Promise–proof–plan / problem–solution–proof–CTA.** Prefer these when credibility and execution risk dominate.

## Explanatory structures

**SCQA — Situation, Complication, Question, Answer.** Use for a genuine change or tension. Do not invent a complication.

**Pyramid Principle.** Lead with the supported conclusion, group mutually distinct reasons, and place detail beneath them. Do not hide uncertainty to produce a neat answer.

**Claim–evidence–reasoning.** Use when readers need to understand why evidence supports a claim. Distinguish observation from inference.

**Inverted pyramid.** Put the most decision-relevant information first for news, updates, and scan-heavy pages.

## Social and script structures

- **Hook–Value–CTA:** earn attention, deliver the promised value, then request one proportionate action.
- **Problem–Mistake–Fix:** useful when a common error is supported by evidence; avoid belittling users.
- **Myth–Reality–Evidence:** use only when the “myth” is genuinely held and the correction is supported.
- **Context–Conflict–Resolution / ABT (And–But–Therefore):** compress a real tension into a causal narrative.
- **Question–Tension–Answer:** answer promptly; do not withhold essential information as clickbait.
- **Rule of Three:** group for recall when three items reflect the evidence, not as a forced quota.

## Content programme frameworks

**Hero–Hub–Help:** occasional campaign or launch assets, recurring series, and durable answers to audience questions. Do not overinvest in “hero” content while neglecting useful evergreen material.

**See–Think–Do–Care:** serve broad interest, active consideration, action, and existing-customer value. Treat stages as audience states, not a compulsory linear journey.

**PESO:** coordinate paid, earned, shared, and owned distribution. Do not describe sponsored or controlled placement as earned.

**Topic clusters / hub-and-spoke:** connect a substantive central resource to focused supporting content. Avoid thin pages created only to capture keyword variants.

## Selection and validation

1. Define reader state, central idea, available proof, channel, and CTA.
2. Choose one primary structure; add a supporting device only if it solves a separate problem.
3. Draft without exposing framework labels.
4. Remove formulaic transitions and repeated openings.
5. Check that every tension, benefit, proof point, and deadline is true.
6. Validate comprehension, credibility, completion, or qualified action—not framework compliance.

## Misuse guards

Do not manufacture pain, conflict, urgency, curiosity gaps, scarcity, authority, consensus, or transformation. Do not force a hard CTA into low-intent education. A structure that weakens voice or omits material information is the wrong structure.
