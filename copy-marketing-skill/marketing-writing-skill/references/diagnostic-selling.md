# Diagnostic selling

Use for sales messaging, service pages, discovery prompts, and complex or considered purchases where premature claims reduce trust.

## Diagnostic sequence

1. Establish only the situation facts needed to understand fit.
2. Surface a relevant problem or friction.
3. Explore implications: cost, delay, risk, opportunity loss, and downstream effects.
4. Let the desired value emerge by clarifying what improvement would enable.
5. Connect the offer to that value with proof and a proportionate next step.

Implication questions should deepen a verified issue, not inflate a trivial one. Value questions should help the buyer articulate useful outcomes, not script praise.

## Copy moves

Use sales copy to mirror the diagnostic logic: recognizable situation, consequential problem, desired change, mechanism, evidence, objections, CTA. For outbound email, use one well-supported problem hypothesis and invite correction.

## Misuse guards

Do not overwhelm readers with questions. Do not assume a problem exists because the product solves it. Do not weaponize implications or manufacture fear.
