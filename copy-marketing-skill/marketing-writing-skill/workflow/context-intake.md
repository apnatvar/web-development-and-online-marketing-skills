# Context intake

## Build the minimum viable brief

Extract known facts before asking anything. Track:

| Field | Question it resolves |
|---|---|
| Audience | Who must understand or act? |
| Awareness | What do they already know about the problem, solution, and offer? |
| Stated problem | What friction is named? |
| Underlying problem | What cause, consequence, or decision barrier sits beneath it? |
| Desired outcome | What change does the audience want? |
| Offer | What product or service can credibly help? |
| Alternatives | What would they use or do otherwise, including inaction? |
| Differentiation | Why choose this for this situation? |
| Positioning | What category or frame makes its value legible? |
| Proof | What verified evidence supports claims? |
| Objections | What blocks belief or action? |
| Desired action | What should the reader do next? |
| Platform | Where will the message appear? |
| Tone | What should the voice feel like, and what samples define it? |
| Length | What limit or depth is useful? |
| Business objective | What business result should the asset support? |

## Ask selectively

Ask a question only if different plausible answers would materially change audience, claim, structure, proof, CTA, or platform execution. Batch at most three high-impact questions. If progress is safe, state a narrow assumption and draft.

Prioritize missing facts in this order:

1. audience and action;
2. offer truth and proof;
3. problem, outcome, and awareness;
4. alternatives and differentiation;
5. channel constraints and voice.

Do not ask users to repeat context already present. Do not demand a complete template before helping.

## Context status

Mark inputs internally as `verified`, `user-stated`, `inferred`, `unknown`, or `contradictory`. Published claims must rely on verified or user-stated facts. Surface consequential contradictions.
