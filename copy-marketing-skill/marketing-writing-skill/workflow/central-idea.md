# Central idea

Define one message contract before adapting any channel.

## Central-idea card

- **Audience:** specific reader and situation
- **Core tension:** supported problem or decision
- **Main claim:** one complete, defensible sentence
- **Why now:** relevant urgency without fabricated scarcity
- **Mechanism:** how the offer produces the outcome
- **Contrast:** realistic alternative and meaningful difference
- **Proof:** strongest verified support
- **Emotional stake:** human consequence, when supported
- **Action:** proportionate next step
- **Boundaries:** claims or implications to avoid

## Stress test

The idea should be:

- singular enough to summarize in one sentence;
- relevant to the audience's awareness;
- differentiated through substance, not tone;
- supported or explicitly provisional;
- concrete enough to demonstrate;
- broad enough to survive adaptation without changing meaning.

If several ideas compete, choose by business objective and reader need. For multiple valid routes, present separate cards such as “risk-reduction,” “time-to-value,” and “specialist-fit,” each with a tradeoff.

## Adaptation invariant

Platforms may change hook, sequence, depth, evidence density, CTA, formatting, and vocabulary. They must not silently change audience, promise, mechanism, proof strength, or strategic contrast.
