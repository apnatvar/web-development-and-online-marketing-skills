# Problem diagnosis

Separate five layers:

1. **Symptom:** visible friction (“reporting takes too long”).
2. **Immediate cause:** mechanism producing it (“data is copied among three systems”).
3. **Underlying constraint:** structural issue (“no shared definition or owner”).
4. **Consequence:** credible effect (“review starts late and errors need rework”).
5. **Decision barrier:** why the audience has not changed (“migration risk outweighs the apparent gain”).

Then identify the audience's desired progress and the business's objective. They may overlap but are not interchangeable.

## Diagnosis protocol

- Write the stated problem in the user's words.
- List plausible underlying explanations as hypotheses, not facts.
- Test each against supplied evidence, customer behavior, alternatives, and objections.
- Choose the narrowest supported diagnosis that makes strategic choices possible.
- Identify what the offer can and cannot change.
- Define the pivotal message challenge in one sentence.

Challenge a brief when it targets a symptom the offer does not solve, assumes desire without evidence, treats a feature as an outcome, or requests a CTA too large for audience readiness.

## Output for critique modes

Rank findings by impact:

1. strategic mismatch;
2. unsupported or risky claim;
3. missing logic or proof;
4. channel mismatch;
5. style and polish.

Quote or point to the exact element, explain its consequence, then propose a concrete correction.
