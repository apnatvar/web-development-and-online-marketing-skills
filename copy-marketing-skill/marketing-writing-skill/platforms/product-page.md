# Product page

## Purpose and structure

Use to help a buyer understand a specific product, judge fit, compare alternatives, and act.

1. Product identity, best-fit use, and primary outcome.
2. Visual or concrete product understanding.
3. Key capabilities grouped by buyer job.
4. Benefits connected to mechanisms.
5. Specifications, compatibility, or requirements.
6. Proof and comparison.
7. Objections: setup, price, returns, security, support, or availability as relevant.
8. Purchase, trial, demo, or contact CTA.

## Execution

- **Opening:** Name the product and value plainly; retain necessary category terms.
- **Length:** Scale with complexity, risk, and consideration. Put essential decision facts high.
- **Density:** Use scannable sections, tables for exact comparisons, and prose for interpretation.
- **CTA:** Reflect transaction model and disclose the next step.
- **Formatting:** Keep variant names, specifications, price units, and availability unambiguous.
- **Hashtags:** None.
- **SEO:** Match product and category intent; write unique descriptive copy rather than manufacturer duplication.
- **Metadata:** Include distinct title and description; provide structured-data fields only from verified facts.

## Prohibited patterns

No feature dumping, unsupported “best” claims, buried exclusions, invented review summaries, ambiguous pricing, or benefits unrelated to product capability.

## Edit

Check consistency among title, variant, imagery, specs, availability, price, and CTA. Separate verified specifications from persuasive interpretation.
