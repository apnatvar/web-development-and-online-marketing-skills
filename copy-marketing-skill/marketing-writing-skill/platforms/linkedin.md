# LinkedIn

## Purpose and structure

Use for professional insight, founder-led perspective, credible education, case-based learning, and conversation.

1. Open with the main claim, tension, concrete observation, or honest admission.
2. Establish why it matters to the named professional audience.
3. Develop one argument through evidence, example, or mechanism.
4. Resolve with a practical implication.
5. Use one low-friction CTA when useful.

## Execution

- **Opening:** Make the first 1–3 lines self-sufficient before truncation. Avoid generic questions and manufactured controversy.
- **Length:** Prefer roughly 600–1,500 characters for a focused post; go longer only when the argument earns it. Confirm current hard limits when critical.
- **Density:** Use short paragraphs, usually 1–3 sentences. Do not put every sentence on its own line.
- **CTA:** Invite a specific response, save, follow, resource request, or next step. Do not stack engagement requests.
- **Formatting:** Plain text should carry the meaning. Use bullets only for genuine lists. Avoid decorative Unicode and excessive emoji.
- **Hashtags:** Usually zero to three relevant tags; omit when they add no discovery value.
- **SEO/metadata:** Not primary. Put searchable topic language naturally in the opening and body.

## Founder-led layer

Use first-person experience only when supplied. Separate “what I observed” from universal advice. Include decision, constraint, mistake, lesson, or tradeoff; do not manufacture vulnerability.

## Prohibited patterns

No “Agree?”, baited contrarianism, false personal stories, line-break ladders, empty moral lessons, fake quotations, or “Here are 10 secrets” without substance.

## Edit

Remove preamble, protect the central claim, test the first three lines, verify anecdotes, and read aloud for natural cadence.
