# Service page

## Purpose and structure

Use to sell expertise, process, confidence, and fit when delivery is partly intangible.

1. Best-fit client, situation, and outcome.
2. Problem diagnosis and cost of the status quo.
3. Service scope and boundaries.
4. Approach or engagement process.
5. Deliverables connected to outcomes.
6. Credibility: cases, expertise, or transparent method.
7. Fit, exclusions, timeline, and common objections.
8. Consultation, assessment, proposal, or purchase CTA.

## Execution

- **Opening:** Describe the client's change, not merely the provider's credentials.
- **Length:** Explain enough process to reduce uncertainty; avoid exhaustive internal methodology.
- **Density:** Use clear stage and deliverable headings. Keep proof adjacent to the relevant promise.
- **CTA:** Match commitment to buying complexity; say who the conversation is for and what happens.
- **Formatting:** Distinguish included, optional, and excluded scope.
- **Hashtags:** None.
- **SEO:** Align with a specific service plus audience, problem, industry, or location when genuinely served.
- **Metadata:** Use a unique service title and outcome-focused description.

## Prohibited patterns

No guaranteed outcomes outside provider control, vague “tailored solutions,” invented client pain, credential dumping, hidden scope, or pressure disguised as consultation.

## Edit

Replace generic capability claims with process, artifact, or evidence. Test whether a qualified prospect can determine fit and next-step risk.
