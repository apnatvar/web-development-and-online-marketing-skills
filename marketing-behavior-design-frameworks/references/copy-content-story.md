# Copy, Content, and Story Frameworks

Choose structure after audience, need, position, proof, and desired action are known. These patterns organise material; they do not supply facts.

## Copy structures

| Framework | Best for | Core sequence | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AIDA | short sales pages, ads, or emails | Attention, Interest, Desire, Action | treating attention stages as a guaranteed buyer journey |
| ACCA | considered or educational offers | Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action | unsupported conviction claims |
| PAS | problem-led copy | Problem, Agitate, Solution | exaggerating distress or anxiety |
| BAB | transformation-led copy | Before, After, Bridge | promising an unproven after-state |
| FAB | technical offers | Feature, Advantage, Benefit | inventing benefits not supported by use |
| PASTOR | long-form sales copy | Problem, Amplify, Story/Solution, Transformation/Testimony, Offer, Response | excessive amplification or fake testimony |
| QUEST | complex audience qualification | Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition | exclusion based on invented persona assumptions |
| 4Us | headline review | Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific | fabricated urgency or uniqueness |
| 4Cs copy check | final editing | Clear, Concise, Compelling, Credible | “compelling” overriding accuracy |
| Message House | cross-channel consistency | central message, pillars, proof | repeating one claim without channel adaptation |
| Claim-Evidence-Reasoning | explanatory or evidence-led writing | claim, support, connection | weak sources or hidden inference |
| SCQA | articles and presentations | Situation, Complication, Question, Answer | manufacturing complication |
| Inverted pyramid | news, help, and web content | conclusion and essentials first | losing necessary nuance |

Use one primary structure. Add a second only when it solves a different layer, such as Message House for consistency plus PAS for one page.

## Content systems

- **Editorial mission:** audience, need, promise, and repeatable content role.
- **Content pillars:** a limited set of strategically distinct themes supported by audience demand and organisational competence.
- **Topic clusters / hub-and-spoke:** one central resource connected to specific supporting content; links must serve readers, not only search engines.
- **Hero-Hub-Help:** tent-pole reach, recurring programming, and evergreen answers.
- **See-Think-Do-Care:** organise content by audience intent rather than assuming a linear funnel.
- **TOFU-MOFU-BOFU:** a simple awareness-to-decision planning shorthand; use carefully for non-linear journeys.
- **PESO:** Paid, Earned, Shared, Owned distribution.
- **RACE:** Reach, Act, Convert, Engage planning and measurement.
- **5A:** Aware, Appeal, Ask, Act, Advocate customer-path view.
- **AARRR:** Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue for product-growth content.
- **Content atomisation:** derive channel-native pieces from a substantive source; do not paste the same copy everywhere.

## Story and script structures

| Structure | Useful for |
| --- | --- |
| Three-act structure | setup, confrontation, resolution in longer scripts |
| Freytag pyramid | dramatic narrative with rising action and climax |
| Hero's Journey | long transformation narratives; usually too heavy for short posts |
| Story Circle | an eight-beat change arc for character-led scripts |
| Pixar Story Spine | rapid cause-and-effect story development |
| ABT: And, But, Therefore | concise context, tension, and consequence |
| STAR / CAR | evidence-led case studies and testimonials |
| Hook-Story-Offer | promotion driven by a relevant narrative |
| Hook-Value-CTA | short educational or social content |
| Hook-Problem-Insight-Payoff-CTA | short explanatory video |
| Problem-Mistake-Fix | corrective educational content |
| Myth-Reality-Evidence | misconception content |

Open loops, curiosity gaps, pattern interrupts, and fear-based hooks are devices, not strategies. The opening must accurately represent the content and must not withhold safety, price, eligibility, or other material information.

## Selection questions

1. Is the reader problem-aware, solution-aware, comparison-ready, or already a customer?
2. Is the goal comprehension, trust, action, learning, retention, or sharing?
3. What proof can be used?
4. Which objection or decision barrier is evidenced?
5. Which structure communicates this with the least distortion?

After writing, remove framework labels and production notes from reader-facing output.
