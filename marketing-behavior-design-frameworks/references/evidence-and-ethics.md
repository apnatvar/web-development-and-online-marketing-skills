# Evidence, Validation, and Ethics

## Evidence classes

- **Empirical theory or effect:** supported by a research programme but still bounded by population, context, measurement, and replication quality.
- **Professional method or standard:** a repeatable process such as STP, journey mapping, Double Diamond, Nielsen heuristics, or WCAG.
- **Practitioner heuristic:** a useful composition or review mnemonic such as AIDA, PAS, 4Us, StoryBrand, or Hero-Hub-Help.

Do not call all three “psychology.” Record the class when it affects confidence.

## Minimum evidence record

For every material recommendation capture:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| observation | what was actually seen or supplied |
| source | research, analytics, interview, content, product, or user claim |
| framework interpretation | how the selected model explains or organises it |
| decision | what should change and why |
| uncertainty | alternative explanations or missing evidence |
| validation | research, usability test, content test, experiment, or monitoring |

Framework fit is not evidence that the recommendation will work.

## Validation choices

- Interviews and contextual inquiry for needs, language, alternatives, and circumstances.
- Surveys for distribution of clearly defined attitudes or self-reports, not causal proof.
- Search/query, support, sales, and community data for recurring expressed demand.
- Message or comprehension tests for positioning and value propositions.
- Card sorting and tree testing for information architecture.
- Moderated or unmoderated task testing for usability.
- Accessibility inspection plus assistive-technology and user testing.
- Controlled experiments for causal comparison when sample, duration, and decision stakes justify them.
- Cohort and funnel analysis for behavioural patterns, with confounding acknowledged.

Define the decision threshold and stopping rule before reading experiment results. Do not optimise only clicks when the user or business outcome is retention, comprehension, safety, or qualified action.

## Known caution areas

- Maslow's fixed hierarchy and universal ordering are contested.
- Herzberg was developed for job attitudes and should not be treated as general consumer law.
- Choice overload depends on option complexity, preferences, and decision support; “fewer is always better” is false.
- Loss aversion is not a licence for fear-based messaging and may vary by context.
- “System 1/System 2” is a simplifying account, not two literal switches in the brain.
- Brand archetypes, AIDA, PAS, StoryBrand, 4Us, Golden Circle, and funnel mnemonics are useful organising heuristics, not predictive science.
- UX “laws” describe tendencies or review principles; user context and testing govern application.

## Manipulation and dark-pattern check

Do not use frameworks to create:

- fake urgency, scarcity, testimonials, authority, popularity, or comparison prices;
- hidden costs, bundled consent, disguised advertising, preselected paid additions, or misleading hierarchy;
- obstructed cancellation, deletion, privacy choices, refunds, support, or refusal;
- shame, coercion, excessive interruption, addictive reward schedules, or pressure aimed at vulnerability;
- interface copy that hides eligibility, risk, recurring charges, data use, or material limitations.

The ethical test is user agency with informed understanding, not whether a technique improves a metric.
