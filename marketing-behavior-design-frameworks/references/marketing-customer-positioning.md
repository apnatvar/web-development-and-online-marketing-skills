# Marketing, Customer Needs, Positioning, and Brand

Use these frameworks to make market and message decisions. Do not fill them with invented customer facts.

## Marketing and growth strategy

| Framework | Use it to | Required inputs | Output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4Ps: Product, Price, Place, Promotion | Audit the core marketing mix | offer, price, distribution, promotion evidence | mix gaps and decisions |
| 7Ps: add People, Process, Physical Evidence | Analyse a service or experience | delivery process, staff/automation roles, trust signals | service-marketing plan |
| 4Cs: Customer, Cost, Convenience, Communication | Reframe the mix from the customer view | customer research and total effort/cost | customer-centred adjustments |
| SAVE: Solution, Access, Value, Education | Plan B2B or complex-service marketing | customer problem, access model, value evidence | education-led mix |
| SOSTAC | Build a controlled plan | situation, objectives, resources, measures | strategy, tactics, action, control |
| RACE: Reach, Act, Convert, Engage | Plan digital lifecycle activity | audience stages, channels, conversion events | channel actions and measures |
| Ansoff Matrix | Compare growth directions | existing/new products and markets | growth-option risks |
| Porter Five Forces | Assess competitive pressure | substitutes, entrants, suppliers, buyers, rivals | market-pressure analysis |
| Blue Ocean Strategy / Strategy Canvas | Search for a different value curve | researched competing factors and unmet value | eliminate-reduce-raise-create hypotheses |

Use SWOT only as a summary of prior evidence. Use TOWS to turn that evidence into actions. Do not begin with unsourced SWOT brainstorming and call it research.

## Segmentation and customer understanding

| Framework | Best use | Guardrail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| STP: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning | choose a valuable audience and intended market position | segments must differ in needs or response, not merely labels |
| ICP | define a best-fit account or customer | validate against retention, value, success, and cost-to-serve evidence |
| Buyer and user personas | preserve differences between purchaser and user context | research-based synthesis, never fictional biography padding |
| Buying committee map | separate user, buyer, approver, influencer, and blocker needs | do not assume titles determine influence |
| Jobs to Be Done | identify progress sought in a specific circumstance | a job is not a feature request or demographic |
| Job Map | break the job into stages independent of a solution | map the job before designing the product path |
| Voice of Customer | collect natural language, priorities, objections, and alternatives | retain source and frequency; one quote is not a segment |
| Needs/benefit segmentation | group by desired outcome or benefit | test whether groups are reachable and meaningfully different |
| Kano Model | separate basic expectations, performance needs, and potential delighters | classifications change with audience and time |
| Empathy map | organise what people say, think, do, and feel | label inference separately from observation |
| Customer journey map | map stages, goals, questions, emotions, and friction | do not confuse a company funnel with the customer journey |
| Service blueprint | connect touchpoints to frontstage and backstage delivery | include operational dependencies and failure points |

## Value proposition and positioning

### Value Proposition Canvas

Map customer jobs, pains, and gains against products/services, pain relievers, and gain creators. Prioritise only important, evidenced needs. The canvas describes a fit hypothesis; customer evidence establishes fit.

### Positioning sequence

1. Define the target and purchase/use situation.
2. Name the category or frame of reference.
3. Establish points of parity required for credibility.
4. Select customer-valued points of difference.
5. Connect each difference to evidence or a reason to believe.
6. Test comprehension and competitive distinctiveness.

Useful tools:

- **Positioning statement:** For [target] in [situation], [offer] is the [category] that [benefit] because [reason to believe]. Keep it internal unless its language also works for customers.
- **POP/POD:** separate expected category qualities from meaningful differentiation.
- **Perceptual map:** plot researched customer perceptions on two relevant attributes; do not choose convenient axes without evidence.
- **Benefit ladder / means-end chain:** feature -> functional consequence -> emotional/social benefit -> underlying value.
- **USP:** express one defensible customer-valued distinction; uniqueness without relevance is weak positioning.
- **Message House:** one central message, supporting pillars, and proof. Use it to maintain consistency across outputs.

## Brand systems

- **Kapferer Brand Identity Prism:** physique, personality, culture, relationship, reflection, self-image.
- **Keller CBBE / Resonance Pyramid:** salience, meaning, response, resonance.
- **Aaker Brand Identity System:** core identity, extended identity, value proposition, relationships, and associations.
- **Brand Key / Brand Pyramid / Brand Onion:** organise audience insight, benefits, proof, values, personality, and essence; implementations vary, so define the version used.
- **Brand architecture:** branded house, house of brands, endorsed or hybrid structures.
- **Distinctive assets:** repeat recognisable names, colours, forms, characters, or sounds consistently; recognition does not prove positioning.
- **Golden Circle:** Why, How, What can organise a purpose narrative, but it does not replace customer value or evidence.
- **Brand archetypes:** a voice-consistency heuristic, not a validated personality diagnosis.

## Selection rules

- Use 7Ps rather than 4Ps when people, delivery process, or trust evidence materially affects the service.
- Use JTBD to discover or articulate situational progress. Use the Value Proposition Canvas later to map validated jobs, pains, and gains to specific offer elements. Do not load both when only one of those decisions is unresolved.
- Use personas for reusable audience context and journey maps for change across touchpoints.
- Use STP before a positioning statement. Use a perceptual map to test position, not invent it.
- Use a Message House to coordinate outputs after positioning is decided.
