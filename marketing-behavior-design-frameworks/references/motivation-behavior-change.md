# Motivation and Behaviour Change

Use motivational models to diagnose why a useful action may or may not occur. Do not label individuals, promise behavioural outcomes, or turn a model into pressure.

## Needs and motivation

| Model | Decision it supports | Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Self-Determination Theory | support autonomy, competence, and relatedness in content, onboarding, feedback, or community | avoid controlling rewards and false choices |
| Maslow hierarchy | brainstorm possible physiological, safety, belonging, esteem, or growth concerns | the strict universal ladder is contested |
| Herzberg Two-Factor Theory | separate friction-removing basics from motivating qualities | created for workplace attitudes, not universal consumer behaviour |
| McClelland acquired needs | explore achievement, affiliation, and influence as research hypotheses | do not assign fixed personality types |
| ERG Theory | explore existence, relatedness, and growth needs flexibly | broad taxonomy, not predictive segmentation |
| Expectancy Theory | test “Can I do it?”, “Will it produce the outcome?”, and “Do I value the outcome?” | all three require audience evidence |
| Self-efficacy | reduce “I cannot do this” friction with achievable steps, examples, and support | do not imply success is solely individual responsibility |
| Goal-Setting Theory | define suitable goals and feedback | complex unfamiliar tasks may need learning goals, not aggressive targets |
| Flow | align challenge, skill, goals, and feedback | not every task should be gamified |
| Regulatory Focus | explore growth/promotion versus security/prevention framing | test; do not assume a stable audience trait |

## Behaviour diagnosis

### COM-B

Define the target behaviour precisely, then diagnose:

- **Capability:** physical or psychological ability and knowledge;
- **Opportunity:** physical and social conditions;
- **Motivation:** reflective intentions and automatic processes.

Output a barrier/evidence table before selecting an intervention. Copy alone cannot repair missing opportunity, access, product capability, or policy.

### Behaviour Change Wheel

After COM-B diagnosis, consider education, persuasion, incentivisation, coercion, training, restriction, environmental restructuring, modelling, and enablement. For marketing and product work, reject coercive or deceptive interventions and check proportionality, safety, equity, and consent.

### Fogg Behavior Model

Behaviour requires Motivation, Ability, and a Prompt at the same moment. Diagnose failure in this order:

1. Is the requested action specific?
2. Is it easy enough for the user in context?
3. Is the prompt visible and timely?
4. Is more motivation actually needed?

Do not compensate for poor ability or excessive friction with louder persuasion.

### EAST and MINDSPACE

- **EAST:** Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely. Use as a rapid intervention checklist.
- **MINDSPACE:** Messenger, Incentives, Norms, Defaults, Salience, Priming, Affect, Commitments, Ego. Use as hypothesis prompts, not guaranteed effects.

Defaults, norms, urgency, incentives, and prompts must be truthful, reversible where appropriate, and aligned with user interests.

## Progress and habit tools

- **Implementation intentions:** help users form an “If situation X occurs, I will do Y” plan.
- **Goal-gradient / endowed progress:** show only real progress and meaningful milestones.
- **Habit loop:** examine cue, routine, and reward; it is a shorthand, not a full theory of habit.
- **Transtheoretical Model:** adapt information to contemplation, preparation, action, and maintenance; stages are not always linear.
- **BCT taxonomy:** name active techniques such as feedback, prompts, goal setting, action planning, or social support so an intervention can be evaluated.
