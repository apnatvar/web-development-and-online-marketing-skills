# Audience, Positioning, and Content Frameworks

Use this reference when visibility tactics depend on a business or audience decision. Select one primary framework for each decision; a list of framework names is not a strategy. Where inputs are missing, state a hypothesis and the research needed to validate it.

## Selection and deliverables

| Decision | Framework | Required inputs | Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who should the site target? | STP | defensible segments, needs, value, reachability, business fit | target segment, exclusion, positioning implication |
| What progress is the audience seeking? | JTBD + VoC | triggering situation, desired progress, alternatives, direct customer language | job statement, question family, evidence gaps |
| Why should this source or offer be selected? | Value Proposition Canvas + positioning | jobs/pains/gains, category, competitors/alternatives, proof | promise, points of parity/difference, reason to believe |
| What should exist across the journey? | Customer journey + See–Think–Do–Care | stages, questions, channels, pages, next actions | journey-to-page map and content gaps |
| How should a subject become a durable content system? | Topic clusters + Hero–Hub–Help | topic boundary, distinct intents, expertise, evidence and capacity | hub/spoke architecture, format mix, internal links, maintenance rule |
| How should uncertain opportunities be developed? | Double Diamond / Design Thinking | observations, constraints, alternatives, validation access | discover/define evidence, options, pilot, test plan |
| Why might visibility fail to convert? | COM-B; optionally EAST or Fogg B=MAP | observed user task, capability, opportunity, motivation, effort and prompt | barrier hypothesis and ethical experiment |

## STP, JTBD, and Voice of Customer

Segment by materially different needs, situations, buying roles, geography, constraints, or economics—not by superficial demographic stereotypes. Target a segment only when the organization can serve it and provide relevant evidence. Positioning should state who the offer is for, the category or alternative, the primary benefit, the meaningful difference, and the reason to believe.

Write a JTBD hypothesis as:

> When [situation], I want to [make progress], so I can [outcome].

Capture current alternatives, decision criteria, anxieties, and desired outcomes. Use VoC from interviews, support logs, sales calls, reviews, surveys, community discussions, or search/query data with provenance. Do not label invented personas or agent-written copy as VoC.

Translate this evidence into:

- target and excluded audiences;
- real question families and vocabulary;
- page purpose and search/answer intent;
- proof requirements;
- one page versus separate-page decisions.

Do not create separate URLs merely because segments or keywords have different labels. A separate page needs meaningfully different intent, offer, evidence, constraints, or experience.

## Value proposition and positioning

Use the Value Proposition Canvas to compare customer jobs, pains, and gains with products/services, pain relievers, and gain creators. Then produce a concise positioning hypothesis with:

```text
For [target] who [need/situation], [offer] is a [category/frame]
that [primary benefit]. Unlike [credible alternative], it [difference]
because [reason to believe].
```

Establish points of parity needed for category credibility before points of difference. Map each claim to first-party experience, product data, customer evidence, independent sources, or an explicit evidence gap. Do not use unsupported “best,” “leading,” guaranteed outcome, or competitor claims.

## Journey and content-system design

Use See–Think–Do–Care to organize questions before, during, and after a decision. It is not a mandatory linear funnel; people may enter or move nonlinearly.

For each relevant stage record:

- user situation and question;
- required answer and proof;
- suitable page or format;
- current URL or gap;
- internal-link and next-action role;
- search, citation, brand, and business success signal.

Use topic clusters when a hub plus distinct spokes improves understanding and navigation. Each URL needs a distinct intent and standalone value. Use Hero–Hub–Help to balance high-investment assets, repeatable editorial series, and durable task answers, but gate the plan by expertise and maintenance capacity.

Choose content structures by task: inverted pyramid for direct answers, SCQA for explanation, Claim–Evidence–Reasoning for recommendations, 5W1H for completeness, and Problem–Evidence–Solution–Proof–Action for supported commercial pages. Do not impose a formula when it makes prose repetitive or delays the answer.

## Discovery and validation

Use the Double Diamond to separate:

1. **Discover:** query data, SERPs, analytics, support/sales evidence, customer research, competitors, and content inventory.
2. **Define:** target, job, visibility-layer constraint, opportunity, and success condition.
3. **Develop:** alternative architectures, content types, proof sources, and distribution routes.
4. **Deliver:** a small pilot with indexability gates, acceptance tests, measurement, and stop/scale rules.

A workshop, template, or keyword list is not validation. Prefer observed behaviour, first-party evidence, usability research, and controlled cohort comparisons.

## Behaviour, choice, and conversion

Use COM-B to distinguish capability, opportunity, and motivation barriers after a page earns relevant visibility. EAST or Fogg B=MAP may help shape a test around ease, salience, social context, timing, ability, or prompts. Do not diagnose motivation from traffic or conversion rate alone.

Behavioural-economics concepts can improve clarity when used ethically:

- reduce choice overload through clear groups and criteria;
- disclose a truthful reference point for price or value;
- reduce ambiguity about effort, fit, privacy, renewal, and next steps;
- use reversible, disclosed defaults that benefit the user;
- use only genuine and representative social proof or scarcity.

Reject fabricated reviews or popularity, false deadlines, hidden defaults, confirm-shaming, obstructive cancellation, fear without efficacy, and loss framing that hides material facts. Search visibility does not justify manipulative conversion design.

## Application record and misuse check

For each framework-backed initiative, record:

```text
Decision and visibility layer:
Framework selected and why:
Inputs and evidence:
Conclusion or hypothesis:
Page/content implication:
Proof and indexability gate:
User benefit and possible harm:
Acceptance test and metrics:
Unknowns, limitation, and stop/scale rule:
```

Do not present Maslow, archetypes, “neuromarketing,” personality types, or isolated cognitive biases as deterministic explanations of customers. Do not claim a framework improves rankings. Its value is a clearer decision, traceable evidence, and a testable implementation.
