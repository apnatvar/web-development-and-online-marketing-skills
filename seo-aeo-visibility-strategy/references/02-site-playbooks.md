# Site-type and maturity playbooks

Use these as starting priorities. Change the order when direct evidence shows a different bottleneck.

## Ecommerce

1. Technical index control, category architecture, and facet rules.
2. Product and variant data consistency across pages, schema, and feeds.
3. Unique product data, useful images, reviews, comparison information, and commercial category guidance.
4. Editorial buying guides that link into categories and products.
5. Visual search readiness and current merchant policy compliance.
6. Linkable assets or digital PR based on proprietary category or sales data.

## Agency or professional services

1. One strong page per real service.
2. Industry and use-case pages only where delivery differs materially.
3. Case studies covering inputs, process, outcomes, and limitations.
4. Comparison, cost, implementation, and migration content around buyer objections.
5. Team/author profiles and clear Organization or LocalBusiness identity.
6. Original research or teardowns that can earn references.
7. Local SEO only where geography materially affects demand.

## Marketing or brochure site

1. Clarify the entity, audience, offer, proof, and next action.
2. Strengthen product or service landing pages before expanding the blog.
3. Add evidence pages: case studies, customers, methodology, policies, and useful FAQs.
4. Build one or two focused topic clusters around real buying problems.
5. Earn relevant partner and industry mentions.

## Blog or publisher

1. Establish editorial specialization and navigable topic architecture.
2. Invest in original reporting, data, and first-hand analysis.
3. Make authorship, editorial policy, corrections, and sourcing transparent.
4. Maintain refresh and consolidation routines.
5. Improve internal links and archive discoverability.
6. Add image, video, or community formats when they improve the subject.
7. Reject scaled summaries that add no information gain.

## SaaS or software

1. Core feature and use-case pages.
2. Real integration and compatibility pages.
3. Alternatives, comparisons, migration, implementation, and pricing guidance.
4. Intentionally indexed product documentation.
5. Templates, tools, and calculators as linkable assets.
6. Credible review-site and ecosystem presence.
7. Privacy-safe benchmark data derived from product usage where appropriate.

## Local business

1. Accurate business profile, address/service area, hours, categories, and services.
2. Location or service pages with real local proof.
3. Genuine review acquisition and response process.
4. LocalBusiness markup and consistent core details.
5. Local partnerships, press, associations, and current imagery/catalog information.

## Priority sequence by domain stage

| Stage | New domain | Established domain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Indexability, entity clarity, analytics, and webmaster-tool setup | Technical/index audit and canonical/duplicate cleanup |
| 2 | Core commercial pages | Identify decaying and cannibalizing pages |
| 3 | One focused topic cluster | Refresh and consolidate proven URLs |
| 4 | Original proof, case studies, or data | Redistribute internal links |
| 5 | Selective link earning and PR | Modernize schema and entity signals |
| 6 | BOFU comparison and use-case expansion | Fill demonstrated topic or intent gaps |
| 7 | AI crawler checks and representative prompt monitoring | Analyze AI visibility and expand reputation |

For a very new domain, resist the urge to look large. A compact corpus with clear specialization, useful internal links, real evidence, and a few earned references is more defensible than hundreds of lightly differentiated pages.
