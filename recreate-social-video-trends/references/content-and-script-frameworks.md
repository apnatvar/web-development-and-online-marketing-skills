# Content, Positioning, and Script Frameworks

Use this reference when choosing what a video should say, how it fits a content program, or how to adapt a trend to an audience. Do not load it for a purely technical render correction.

Frameworks guide choices; they are not evidence that a message will work. The labels below distinguish research-grounded models from practitioner heuristics. Use audience research and observable results to validate the selected approach.

## Start with the audience and position

### Jobs to Be Done — practitioner research lens

Define the audience need as:

> When [situation], I want to [make progress], so I can [outcome].

Record the obstacle, current alternative, decision criteria, anxiety, and words the audience actually uses. A trend is relevant only if its editing and narrative grammar help this job. Do not infer sensitive needs from appearance or demographic stereotypes.

### Positioning statement — practitioner strategy tool

Use this internal sentence:

> For [audience in a situation], [offer or idea] is the [category] that [benefit], because [verifiable difference or proof].

Convert it into a **message house**: one claim, two or three supporting points, and proof for each. Use a **benefit ladder** to move from feature to practical effect to outcome, stopping before unsupported emotional claims.

## Place the video in a content system

These are planning heuristics, not rules about how every audience behaves.

- **Hero-Hub-Help:** use Hero for infrequent campaign moments, Hub for a repeatable series, and Help for durable answers to audience questions. A copied trend is not automatically Hero content.
- **See-Think-Do-Care:** distinguish broad category awareness, active consideration, an immediate action, and existing-customer support. Match the CTA and proof burden to the stage; do not claim that a viewer moves linearly through all four.
- **Content pillars:** maintain a small set of themes tied to audience needs and brand competence. Reject a trend that cannot strengthen a pillar unless the experiment has a defined purpose.
- **Content atomisation:** derive platform-specific clips from one substantial source while preserving context. Do not crop a nuanced claim into a misleading sound bite.

For a trend candidate, record its content role, audience job, message pillar, desired response, and why the format improves comprehension or recall. “It is viral” is not a strategy.

## Select the smallest script structure that fits

The following are practitioner heuristics. Do not force every video into all stages or delay useful information only to create retention.

| Content job | Structure | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fast practical lesson | **Hook-Value-CTA** | Name the relevant tension, deliver the value, offer a proportionate next step |
| Correct a common error | **Problem-Mistake-Fix** | Show the problem, explain the actual mistake, demonstrate the fix |
| Evidence-led correction | **Myth-Reality-Evidence** | State the belief fairly, give the accurate account, show the source or demonstration |
| Compact narrative | **ABT** | Establish context with “and,” tension with “but,” consequence with “therefore” |
| Case or transformation | **Context-Conflict-Resolution** | Show a real starting state, obstacle, action, and qualified result |
| Product explanation | **Feature-Effect-Benefit-Proof** | Connect capability to the user's outcome and substantiate it |
| Longer character-led story | **Three-act structure** or **Story Circle** | Use setup, confrontation, and resolution only when genuine change supports the idea |

Apply the **Rule of One** when possible: one viewer, problem, promise, and primary action. Not every useful video needs a conversion CTA; a complete answer or a request to save/reference it may be the right ending.

## Design attention without manipulation

### Elaboration Likelihood Model — research-grounded model

Treat involvement and attention as hypotheses. For consequential, technical, or expensive decisions, allow time for reasoning, qualifications, demonstrations, and sources. For low-attention discovery, simplify the path and use recognizable cues, but do not let production polish substitute for evidence.

### Narrative transportation — research-grounded account

A coherent story can change how an audience processes a message. Use specificity, causality, and a believable point of view. Label reenactments, composites, simulations, and hypothetical results. Do not fabricate testimonials or present a staged outcome as spontaneous.

### Self-Determination Theory — research-grounded model

When relevant, support autonomy with non-coercive options, competence with achievable instruction and feedback, and relatedness with a credible human voice. Avoid copy that mocks the viewer, threatens exclusion, or makes belonging conditional on purchase.

### Cognitive and perceptual hypotheses

- Use the **serial-position effect** to put essential context and the final action where they remain noticeable; repeat only what matters.
- Use the **Von Restorff effect** sparingly: one visually distinct element can direct attention, while constant novelty destroys hierarchy.
- Reduce **cognitive load** by aligning spoken words, captions, visuals, and cuts. Do not stack unrelated motion, dense text, and sound cues.
- Run a **reactance check**: remove controlling language, guilt, bait-and-switch hooks, and implied penalties that are not real.

These effects vary by context. They justify a test, not a promise of retention.

## Behavioural-economics guardrails

Use behavioural concepts only to make truthful choices easier to understand:

| Concept | Acceptable use | Do not do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Framing | Present a true benefit or tradeoff in audience language | Hide material context or alternate outcomes |
| Anchoring | Show a real benchmark, original price, or baseline | Invent or inflate a comparison point |
| Loss aversion | Explain a specific, supportable consequence | Use fear, shame, or vague threatened loss |
| Social proof | Show permissioned, representative results with context | Fake engagement, testimonials, comments, or popularity |
| Scarcity | State a verified capacity, inventory, or deadline | Use evergreen countdowns or false urgency |
| Choice overload | Group options and provide decision criteria | Conceal alternatives or obstruct refusal |

Do not reproduce a trend's manipulation because it appears to perform well. Engagement is a noisy signal and may reward confusion, outrage, accidental replays, or controversy rather than useful communication.

## Framework-informed deliverable

When script strategy is in scope, add a compact rationale to the beat sheet:

1. audience job and evidence source;
2. positioning and proof;
3. content-system role;
4. selected script structure;
5. attention or motivation hypothesis;
6. ethical risk and guardrail;
7. observable success and harm metrics.

Test the actual hypothesis, not the framework label. Pair completion, qualified clicks, saves, or conversions with negative feedback, hides, unfollows, misunderstandings, and accessibility checks. A retention lift does not excuse a misleading hook.
