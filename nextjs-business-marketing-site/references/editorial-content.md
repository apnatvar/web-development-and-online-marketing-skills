# Editorial content

Create articles, guides, comparisons and case studies that answer a real reader question and contribute useful expertise. The goal is an argument or task the reader can use, not a quota of posts or a keyword phrase padded into paragraphs. Factual accuracy and publication state are required; topic, search intent and audience choices need evidence; format, voice and length should fit the project. There is no universal reading-time or word-count target. Inspect the site’s content model, voice, existing pages, links, images, structured data and publication state before drafting. Follow the repository’s conventions instead of importing a generic article schema.

## Select the format and angle

Use an article or field note for a timely development, observation or argument. Use a guide for durable, structured instruction that helps someone complete a task or make a decision. Use a case study for real implementation and its evidence. Use a comparison when a reader must choose among genuinely distinct alternatives. A topic can merit coverage before search demand grows if the business has expertise, customers need an explanation, or useful interpretation strengthens a relevant subject area. Trendiness alone is not enough.

Define the intended reader, question, central claim, distinctive contribution, available evidence and relevant next page. Check whether an existing article already serves the intent. Improve or extend it where that helps more than a new near-duplicate. Keep experiments distinct in substance and record their audience and purpose in internal editorial notes. Do not publish pages that merely swap an offer label or keyword.

## Develop a progressive argument

Turn an outline into a connected explanation, not a transcription. A useful sequence is:

1. State the question or claim and give the reader a reason to continue.
2. Explain the situation and why it matters.
3. Develop the mechanism, distinction or decision that answers the question.
4. Add a practical workflow, example, tradeoff or comparison.
5. Address evidence, limitations, human review and ownership.
6. End with the implication or next action without repeating the whole article.

Each section should answer a question raised by the previous one. Use a descriptive main heading, specific section headings and nested headings only when they improve navigation. A single H1 is a common pattern, not a ranking guarantee. Adapt the structure and length to the subject and reader rather than applying a fixed template or word target. Avoid generic scene-setting, repetitive conclusions, keyword repetition and claims that could belong to any business. Use tables and lists when they make a task or choice easier to understand. Headings should make sense in a table of contents or search result.

Research factual, unstable, specialized or consequential claims. Prefer primary sources such as official documentation, research papers, original datasets and direct case materials. Link a small number of authoritative sources where the evidence matters. Distinguish a source’s findings from your interpretation. Separate observed evidence, internal observations, opinion, hypothesis, forecast and proposed application. Preserve original context and limitations. Never invent statistics, clients, results, experience, citations, product capabilities or demand.

Label hypothetical scenarios near their introduction. Do not present expected impact as a result. For industry workflow studies, begin with a recognizable job, customer-acquisition task or operational bottleneck. Explain the current process, repeated effort or delay, smallest useful change, and where software or automation may help. Include required information, human decisions, exception handling, implementation constraints and a practical evaluation method. Do not assume everyone in an industry shares the same problem.

When external case studies inform a hypothetical scenario, attribute them accurately and preserve their context and measured outcomes. Clearly distinguish their observed results from the proposed application. Invite the reader to an existing offer only when it is a natural next step; do not disguise a sales page as an educational article.

Useful comparison articles apply the same decision to each option. Define the reader’s use case and compare meaningful dimensions such as fit, effort, ownership, maintenance, security, cost assumptions and constraints. Keep criteria stable. Separate verified specifications from editorial judgment, and explain missing information rather than filling gaps. Avoid shallow “best tools” lists without a credible evaluation basis.

## Prepare publication metadata and review

Use the repository’s established content and metadata model. Keep visible title and main heading, description, canonical URL, social metadata, image and alt text, publication and modification dates, structured data and sitemap entry consistent. Inspect root-inherited title templates, canonical and Open Graph defaults alongside page-level overrides across representative routes, then verify the rendered head. Set dates truthfully; only update modification dates after substantive edits. Add relevant internal links where they help the reader move from a problem to an offer, proof, guide or tool. Do not link merely to manipulate rankings.

Read the draft from beginning to end. Remove repetition, verify that the introduction makes a fair promise and each section delivers it, and preserve necessary qualifications. Apply any available editorial or humanization pass after the facts are sound, then review edits for precision, citations, terminology, voice and technical meaning. Editing for naturalness is not permission to invent personal experience or claims.

## Separate publication states and channels

Keep research and drafts distinct from approved content. Use one publication eligibility rule for the direct article route, content index, public API, RSS/feed and sitemap so drafts and not-yet-due items cannot leak through another surface. For example, require a published state and a publication instant that is due. Store scheduled instants with an explicit timezone. A future date alone does not schedule release: provide a deployment, scheduled job, cache expiry or revalidation mechanism that updates each surface at the intended time. Follow existing workflow states without adding unnecessary process machinery. Publication requires the user’s request or existing explicit authorization. Drafting a platform adaptation does not authorize posting, outreach or publishing there.

Keep website content and required rendering assets in the website repository. Store off-site drafts and publication assets in each channel’s designated location, as project instructions specify. Avoid redundant campaign drafts in the website repo. Adapt each version to its platform while preserving sources and meaning. Extra channel deliverables are optional unless the user or project workflow asks for them.

## Lessons from iteration

Progressive structure, primary-source research, evidence boundaries and a complete final read-through improve editorial reliability. Practical workflow stories benefit from explicit hypotheticals, human checks and evaluation criteria. Those are reusable methods; a specific industry backlog, article length, platform quota or channel-storage path belongs to the project that chose it.
