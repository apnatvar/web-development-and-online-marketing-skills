# Strategy, Behaviour, and Content Frameworks

Use this reference when the hard question is what a page should do or how it should help a person—not for routine metadata checks. Select one primary framework for the decision and add a second only when it answers a different question. Frameworks organize evidence; they do not substitute for research or prove ranking impact.

## Selection map

| Decision | Framework | Minimum inputs | Required output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Which audience and need should a page serve? | STP + Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) | defensible segments, situation, desired progress, constraints, alternatives | target segment, job statement, exclusion, evidence gaps |
| What should the page promise and prove? | Value Proposition Canvas + positioning statement | customer jobs/pains/gains, category, alternatives, differentiator, proof | page promise, points of parity/difference, reason to believe |
| Which pages are needed across discovery and decision? | Customer journey + See–Think–Do–Care | stages, questions, channels, anxieties, current pages, desired actions | stage-to-page map, content gap, next useful action |
| How should a subject be covered? | Topic cluster + Hero–Hub–Help | subject boundary, recurring questions, search intent, expertise, maintenance capacity | hub/spoke map, distinct intent per URL, link plan, update owner |
| Why is a useful action not happening? | COM-B, then EAST or Fogg B=MAP | observed barrier, user capability, opportunity, motivation, prompt and effort | diagnosed barrier, smallest ethical intervention, measurement |
| Can people find and understand the page? | Mental-model mapping, card sorting, tree testing, information scent | user labels, navigation paths, representative tasks | proposed hierarchy/labels and a validation plan |
| Is the interface readable and operable? | Nielsen heuristics + WCAG POUR + cognitive-load review | rendered interface, device states, keyboard/screen-reader evidence, task | evidenced issue, affected user/task, repair, verification |
| Is a visual presentation helping comprehension? | Gestalt principles + visual hierarchy | page layout, grouping, reading order, contrast and spacing | grouping/hierarchy finding tied to task completion |
| Is a claim sufficiently supported? | Claim–Evidence–Reasoning | claim, primary evidence, inference, limitation | supported conclusion or a request for evidence |

## Audience, need, and positioning

### STP and JTBD

Use segmentation to identify materially different needs, not to create near-duplicate landing pages. Choose a target only when the site's offer and evidence fit it. Write the job as: “When [situation], I want to [motivation/progress], so I can [outcome].” Include functional, emotional, or social dimensions only when research supports them.

For each proposed page or family, record:

- target segment and excluded audience;
- triggering situation and desired progress;
- present alternative or workaround;
- decision criteria, anxiety, and evidence source;
- why one page can or cannot serve adjacent segments.

Do not infer motivations from demographics, invent persona details, or multiply URLs for keyword variants.

### Value proposition and positioning

Translate the target job into a truthful promise. State the target, category/frame of reference, primary benefit, meaningful difference, and reason to believe. A page must establish expected category information—points of parity—before relying on points of difference.

Output a one-sentence positioning hypothesis, three or fewer proof pillars, and the evidence needed for each. Unsupported superlatives, vague “AI-powered” claims, false comparisons, and invented customer outcomes fail this model.

Use a benefit ladder to connect **feature → functional consequence → relevant outcome**. Stop at the highest outcome evidence supports; do not manufacture an emotional transformation.

## Journey and content architecture

Map real questions across **See** (broad relevant audience), **Think** (active consideration), **Do** (decision/action), and **Care** (existing users/customers). This is a planning lens, not a mandatory four-page funnel. A single page may serve multiple stages if intent and experience remain coherent.

For each stage, define the user's question, current evidence, best content type, page/URL, next useful action, and success signal. Then use a topic cluster only where a hub and distinct spokes improve navigation and coverage. Merge synonyms that require the same answer. Reject a spoke that exists only to capture a keyword permutation.

Use Hero–Hub–Help to balance occasional major assets, repeatable editorial series, and durable task answers. Do not turn publishing cadence into a quality target; maintenance capacity is a gate.

## Behaviour and conversion

Use COM-B before proposing persuasion:

- **Capability:** can the person understand and perform the action?
- **Opportunity:** does the interface or context permit it?
- **Motivation:** does the value, trust, or timing support it?

If the issue is execution friction, EAST can test whether the action is easy, attractive, social, and timely. Fogg B=MAP is useful for checking whether motivation, ability, and a prompt coincide. Neither model proves why a metric is low without user or analytics evidence.

Behavioural-economics concepts are diagnosis and experiment prompts:

- reduce choice overload by grouping or recommending on explicit criteria;
- use reversible defaults that match the user's likely interest;
- provide a truthful comparison reference for price or value;
- reduce ambiguity with clear terms, effort, privacy, and next steps;
- use genuine, representative social proof;
- state scarcity, deadlines, risks, and typical outcomes only when verifiable.

Never use hidden defaults, obstructive cancellation, disguised advertising, fake scarcity, fabricated social proof, shame, fear without an effective response, or loss framing that conceals material facts. Record the user benefit, possible harm, reversibility, disclosure, and metric before recommending a behavioural intervention.

## Information architecture, design, and comprehension

Design Thinking or the Double Diamond may structure uncertain work: discover evidence, define the problem, develop alternatives, and deliver/test the smallest suitable change. Do not treat workshop completion as validation.

Apply these checks to representative tasks:

1. **Information scent:** do labels predict what follows?
2. **Recognition over recall:** are choices and context visible when needed?
3. **Consistency and user control:** do familiar patterns, undo, cancel, and back paths work?
4. **Choice and target effort:** are decisions grouped and important targets reachable without oversimplifying the task?
5. **Gestalt and hierarchy:** do proximity, similarity, contrast, and spacing express the intended groups and reading order?
6. **Cognitive load:** remove irrelevant material, segment complex explanations, and put labels or help beside what they explain.
7. **WCAG POUR:** verify that content is perceivable, operable, understandable, and robust; do not claim accessibility from visual inspection alone.

Use card sorting to discover grouping, tree testing to test findability, and task/usability testing to validate interaction. A heuristic finding should name the observed evidence and the user consequence, not merely cite a law.

## Content construction

Choose the structure that matches the reader's task:

- **Inverted pyramid:** lead with the answer for news, support, and direct informational needs.
- **SCQA:** situation, complication, question, answer for explanatory or strategic material.
- **Claim–Evidence–Reasoning:** for recommendations and factual arguments.
- **Problem–Evidence–Solution–Proof–Action:** for commercial pages where a real problem and supported offer exist.
- **5W1H:** check explanatory completeness.
- **Pyramid Principle or MECE:** organize complex material without overlap or gaps.

These are editing structures, not fixed templates. Avoid formulaic openings, artificial agitation, copied competitor structures, filler, buried answers, clickbait open loops, and calls to action that conflict with the page's informational promise.

## Framework application record

For consequential recommendations, include:

```text
Decision:
Selected framework and why:
Evidence/inputs:
Finding:
Recommendation:
Expected user benefit:
SEO/indexability implication:
Risk or ethical check:
Validation method and metric:
Unknowns:
```

Do not present a framework label as evidence. If inputs are missing, produce a hypothesis and research plan rather than a confident prescription.
