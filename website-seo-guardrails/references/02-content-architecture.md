# Content Architecture and Website Inclusions

## Opportunity test

Before creating a page family, define audience, task, business relevance, unique evidence, maintenance owner, internal-link role, success event, and what would make each URL distinct. Reject ideas driven only by keyword volume or page count.

## High-value inclusions

### Core business pages

Create focused home, service, product, pricing/method, about/company, contact, policy, support, and location pages as genuinely needed. State capabilities, limits, process, evidence, pricing context, ownership, contact routes, and next steps accurately. Avoid vague “best” claims and near-duplicate service/location doorways.

### Tools and calculators

Build tools that complete a real task. Provide an indexable landing page only when the tool works, explains inputs/outputs and methodology, handles errors, is accessible, and offers value beyond ads or lead capture. Add examples, limitations, privacy treatment, version/change notes, and relevant documentation. A fake or misleading tool is a serious policy risk.

### Courses and learning hubs

Design course → module → lesson architecture with clear outcomes, prerequisites, authors/reviewers, exercises, original examples, navigation, and update ownership. Index overview pages first. Index lessons selectively when they stand alone and offer substantive authored value. Keep generated outlines, answer keys, duplicate transcripts, and thin lessons out of search.

### Blogs and editorial content

Use topic clusters aligned with actual expertise and customer questions. Each article needs an original thesis, evidence/experience, accurate sourcing, useful examples, clear authorship/review, and a maintenance rule. Do not publish many lightly varied posts or change dates without substantive updates.

### Directories, glossaries, and knowledge bases

Prefer strong hubs, filters, and searchable datasets over thousands of weak landing pages. Index a detail page only when it contains unique research, firsthand evaluation, decision support, or meaningful data. Keep filter combinations controlled. Cite source licenses and distinguish editorial judgment from vendor claims.

### Case studies and research

Use verified outcomes, methodology, context, constraints, dates, and permissions. Anonymize honestly. Never fabricate clients, testimonials, screenshots, metrics, or citations.

### Comparisons and alternatives

Disclose selection criteria, affiliations, testing method, update date, and limitations. Compare consistent dimensions. Do not impersonate competitors, copy their text, or create “alternative to X” pages at scale without original evidence.

### FAQs

Answer genuine questions where users need them. Avoid duplicating the same FAQ across many pages. FAQ content can aid users, but markup eligibility and display are limited and never guaranteed.

### Original data and media

Publish datasets, templates, diagrams, images, videos, transcripts, and downloadable resources when they improve task completion. Provide stable URLs, accessible alternatives, provenance, licensing, and descriptive landing pages.

## Information architecture

- Organize by user mental model, not keyword permutations.
- Keep important pages within sensible link depth from persistent hubs.
- Use descriptive crawlable `<a href>` links and meaningful anchors.
- Provide breadcrumbs where hierarchy helps.
- Prevent orphan pages and circular dead ends.
- Avoid taxonomy overlap that creates several pages for the same intent.
- Ensure pagination has crawlable sequential links; bots do not click “load more” buttons.

## Editorial template

For every content type define required fields, evidence standard, author/reviewer, schema eligibility, indexability default, internal links, freshness cadence, archiving rule, and QA test. Default generated content to `hold` or `noindex` until reviewed.
