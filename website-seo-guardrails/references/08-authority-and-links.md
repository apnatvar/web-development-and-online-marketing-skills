# Authority and Links

## Earn links through usefulness

Prioritize original research/data, genuinely useful free tools, strong documentation, visual assets, expert analysis, public resources, partnerships, communities, and digital PR relevant to the site's field. Promote selectively to people whose audience benefits.

## Backlink review

Assess relevance, editorial context, page/site quality, placement, anchor naturalness, traffic/audience, link attributes, destination status, and acquisition method. Recover strong broken backlinks through correct one-to-one redirects. Reclaim legitimate unlinked mentions. Do not equate a third-party metric with Google's view.

## Prohibited/risky patterns

Avoid buying ranking links; bulk exchanges; private blog networks; automated comment/forum/profile links; low-quality directories; widgets/themes with forced keyword links; press-release syndication for anchor manipulation; scholarships or fake awards solely for links; and scaled guest posts without editorial value.

Paid, affiliate, sponsored, gifted, or advertorial relationships require clear user disclosure and appropriate link qualification. Do not promise dofollow links.

## Negative SEO and disavow

Do not panic over every unfamiliar backlink. Search engines often ignore spam. Investigate manual actions or a documented history of manipulative link building before considering disavowal; misuse can discard useful signals. Preserve exports and rationale before any irreversible submission.

## Internal authority

Link from relevant high-value hubs to important pages with descriptive anchors. Fix or redirect broken internal destinations, reduce unnecessary chains, keep navigation coherent, and avoid sitewide keyword-stuffed anchors.
