# AI Search Visibility

## Google AI features

Google states that foundational SEO applies to AI Overviews and AI Mode. A supporting page must be indexed and eligible for a snippet; no special schema or AI text file is required. Make important content crawlable, internally discoverable, textually available, people-first, and supported by accurate media and matching structured data.

Do not create pages for every fan-out query, manufacture mentions, or use “AEO/GEO hacks” that become scaled content abuse. Focus on non-commodity information: original experience, primary data, clear methods, useful comparisons, attributable expertise, and direct answers with necessary nuance.

Use `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, or `noindex` when you need to limit Search previews. Review Googlebot and Google-Extended controls separately because Search crawling and some other AI uses are distinct.

## Other assistants and crawlers

Decide crawler access from business, licensing, privacy, capacity, and attribution goals rather than copying a universal robots list. Document the decision. Ensure public facts are consistent across canonical pages, structured data, business listings, feeds, and APIs.

`llms.txt` or public AI documentation may help some consumers discover authoritative material, but is not required for Google AI features and must not be treated as a ranking control. Never expose private data or unpublished claims to make content “AI friendly.”

## Measurement

Use Search Console's available AI/generative reporting and overall Web performance as documented, plus analytics/referrer cohorts, conversions, brand/query trends, Bing tools, and controlled prompts where appropriate. AI citation tests are samples, not exhaustive rank tracking. Record model, locale, date, prompt, personalization limitations, and whether a claim was directly observed.
