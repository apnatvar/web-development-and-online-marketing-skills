# Google Trends for Brand Keyword Decisions

Use Google Trends to learn how search interest changes across time, places, wording, topics, and Google search surfaces. Treat it as comparative evidence for a brand decision, not as absolute keyword volume, conversion proof, or permission to manufacture content.

## Non-negotiable interpretation rules

1. Treat the 0-100 value as a normalized index within the selected comparison, time, geography, category, and search surface. It is not a search count.
2. Remember that 100 marks the highest relative-interest point in the selected result, not universal popularity. A value of 50 means half the indexed interest of that peak within that result.
3. Do not compare scores copied from separately configured charts as though they share one scale. Compare candidates in the same chart or keep a stable anchor term in each comparison batch.
4. Treat a zero as insufficient reported interest under the chosen configuration, not proof that nobody searches. Google suppresses low-volume terms.
5. Treat regional interest as a term's share of all searches in that place. Equal regional scores do not imply equal search counts or market size.
6. Account for sampling, privacy thresholds, statistical noise, and occasional irregular activity. Re-run consequential comparisons and distrust isolated low-volume spikes.
7. Do not infer why interest changed from the graph alone. Check news, campaigns, product releases, outages, seasonality, and other plausible causes.
8. Treat Trends as one input. Validate decisions with Search Console, keyword tools, SERP review, customer language, analytics, sales evidence, and the brand's ability to satisfy the intent.
9. Record the research date and exact filters. Trends is live data and can change.
10. Never promise traffic or ranking because a term is Rising, seasonal, or popular.

Google states that Trends uses a sample of anonymized, categorized, aggregated searches; normalizes it by time and location; and scales it from 0 to 100. Google also notes that very low-volume searches can appear as zero and that internal searches from AI Mode and AI Overviews are excluded. Re-check [10-sources.md](10-sources.md) before consequential current work.

## Define the decision before opening Trends

Write a one-sentence decision such as:

- choose the audience wording for a product page;
- decide which non-branded problem cluster deserves a durable guide;
- distinguish stable demand from a seasonal campaign window;
- choose where to localize an existing useful page;
- compare an exact brand phrase with the recognized brand topic;
- prioritize video, shopping, image, news, or web-search content;
- decide whether a rising query is relevant enough for a small experiment.

State the target audience, market, language, offer, search intent, conversion or learning outcome, available first-hand evidence, and decision deadline. If the decision is undefined, Trends exploration becomes pattern collecting rather than research.

## Build a brand-aware seed set

Separate seeds so different questions do not contaminate each other.

### Branded demand

- exact brand name and recognized brand topic, when available;
- common spellings, spacing, abbreviations, former names, and legitimate transliterations;
- brand plus product, service, pricing, reviews, login, support, documentation, alternatives, careers, or location;
- individual product names and product-family names;
- founder/expert name only when it is genuinely part of brand discovery.

Use brand-plus terms to identify navigational needs and reputation or support gaps. Do not create public pages merely to capture sensitive, defamatory, private, or misleading combinations.

### Non-branded demand

- category and subcategory;
- customer problem and desired outcome;
- job to be done and use case;
- audience or industry wording;
- comparison, alternative, compatibility, integration, template, calculator, course, guide, and how-to language;
- regional terms and language variants actually used by the intended audience.

### Context and controls

- established category terms that provide a readable baseline;
- relevant competitors only for market context and truthful comparison research;
- known events, launches, or seasonal moments that may explain spikes.

Do not use competitor names to impersonate, confuse, stuff metadata, or generate thin alternative pages. Trademark and comparison claims can require professional review.

## Configure each comparison deliberately

Record this query specification before interpreting results:

| Field | Required choice |
| --- | --- |
| Input type | Exact search term or Google-defined topic |
| Geography | Actual served market; drill into subregions only when useful |
| Time | Long enough to answer the decision |
| Category | Use when the wording is ambiguous |
| Surface | Web, Image, News, Google Shopping, or YouTube Search |
| Comparison set | Terms answering the same business question |
| Anchor | Stable reference used across batches, if more candidates exist than one chart can compare |
| Evidence date | Date accessed and exported |

### Term versus topic

Use a **search term** for exact brand wording, spelling, new/niche language, or a specific phrase. It measures the entered wording rather than every synonym or variation.

Use a **topic** for the broader recognized entity or concept across languages and related expressions. Confirm the autocomplete description matches the intended entity. For a brand, compare the exact term and recognized topic when both exist; inspect related queries to detect ambiguity before choosing.

### Time windows

- Use approximately five years to detect durable direction and recurring seasonality.
- Use twelve months to plan the operating calendar.
- Use ninety days to inspect recent movement after a campaign, launch, or market change.
- Use seven or thirty days for tactical monitoring, not as the sole basis of an evergreen SEO strategy.
- Compare with the preceding period and the same period in the prior year where available.
- Align calendar windows when comparing seasonal behavior; partial seasons can mislead.

### Search surface

Use Web Search for ordinary website SEO. Use YouTube Search for video language, Google Shopping for product-shopping interest, Image Search for visual discovery, and News Search for news-driven demand. Do not merge or directly equate indexes from different surfaces.

## Run the research workflow

### 1. Confirm meaning

Search each term/topic separately first. Review related queries, category, and regional pattern. Reject ambiguous inputs or isolate them with an appropriate topic/category.

### 2. Establish the long view

Review a long time range. Classify the pattern as stable, rising, declining, seasonal, event-driven, volatile/uncertain, or below the reporting threshold. Annotate known events but label causal explanations as hypotheses until supported.

### 3. Compare realistic alternatives

Compare terms that could plausibly serve the same page, offer, or decision. Google currently supports up to five groups in a comparison and up to 25 terms within each group. When batching candidates, keep the filters and anchor identical; retain exported data so the method is reproducible.

Do not select a page keyword only because its line is highest. First test whether its intent, meaning, audience, evidence requirements, and brand promise fit the page.

### 4. Examine geography and language

Identify places where relative interest is stronger, then check market size, language, product availability, legal/support readiness, and actual customer evidence. A dark map region is not, by itself, a localization or expansion decision.

For multilingual work, compare the correct language-specific terms and recognized topics. Do not translate keywords literally without validating local meaning and SERP intent.

### 5. Inspect Top and Rising related searches

- **Top** queries show commonly associated searches under the selected configuration.
- **Rising** queries show the strongest growth versus the prior comparable period.
- **Breakout** means growth exceeded 5,000%; it does not reveal the underlying count and can start from a very small base.

Cluster related queries by user intent and meaning. Remove irrelevant entities, news-only spikes, unsafe topics, duplicate wording, and queries the brand cannot answer credibly.

### 6. Investigate spikes and seasonality

Check whether peaks recur, coincide with news or promotions, or vanish when the timeframe changes. For seasonal content, work backward from the recurring rise to allow time for research, production, crawling, indexing, and distribution. Do not fake freshness or republish unchanged pages merely to catch a seasonal spike.

### 7. Validate outside Trends

Use:

- Google Search Console for the site's actual impressions, clicks, pages, countries, devices, and query movement;
- Keyword Planner or a reputable SEO platform for approximate absolute volume and commercial/competitive context;
- live SERP inspection for dominant intent, result type, freshness, and content expectations;
- analytics and CRM/sales records for qualified behavior and commercial outcomes;
- customer interviews, support requests, site search, and sales language for unmet needs;
- the existing content inventory for cannibalization, overlap, and update opportunities.

If a new brand or niche term is below threshold, compare the broader problem/category and use customer evidence. Do not interpret missing Trends data as a failed market.

## Convert observations into brand decisions

Assign every candidate to one of these action classes:

- **Core evergreen:** durable, relevant demand that the brand can satisfy with a strong existing or planned page.
- **Seasonal:** recurring interest with a justified publication/update calendar.
- **Rising experiment:** relevant but uncertain growth; test with a bounded asset or campaign before scaling.
- **Regional/language opportunity:** meaningful relative interest plus operational readiness and corroborating evidence.
- **Watchlist:** insufficient, noisy, ambiguous, or too early; define a review date and trigger.
- **Reject:** irrelevant, misleading, unsafe, news-only, outside the offer, or impossible to serve with distinctive value.

Score only as a decision aid, never as an automatic verdict. Consider brand relevance, user value, intent fit, trend durability, regional fit, evidence strength, current authority, conversion/learning value, production cost, maintenance burden, and spam/cannibalization risk.

Map an approved cluster to the smallest appropriate action: revise an existing page, create a genuinely useful page, add a subsection/FAQ, produce a video or visual, schedule a campaign, localize after validation, or collect more evidence. Do not default every keyword to a new URL.

## Required deliverable

Produce a keyword decision ledger:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Candidate | Exact term/topic and language |
| Brand relationship | Branded, product, category, problem, outcome, comparison, support, or other |
| Trends configuration | Geography, time, category, surface, comparison set, anchor, access date |
| Observation | What the data visibly shows without causal speculation |
| Pattern | Stable, rising, declining, seasonal, event-driven, noisy, or below threshold |
| Related demand | Relevant Top/Rising queries and excluded noise |
| Corroboration | GSC, keyword tool, SERP, customer, analytics, or sales evidence |
| Intent and page fit | User task and existing/new page relationship |
| Decision | Action class, owner, timing, and smallest useful action |
| Confidence/caveat | Limitations, contradictory evidence, and next validation |
| Source | Saved Trends URL/export and any cited chart |

Also provide a short executive summary, rejected ideas with reasons, content/calendar implications, measurement plan, and next refresh date. Attribute reused Trends charts or screenshots to Google Trends and do not cite Trends as though it were Google Ads data.

## Browser and evidence discipline

Use the public Google Trends Explore interface for live research. When browser control is available, inspect and set the term/topic, geography, timeframe, category, and surface visibly; capture the shareable URL and export relevant CSV files. Default to read-only research. Do not alter Google accounts or publish embedded charts without user authorization.

Prefer Google's public UI, exports, and official BigQuery Trends dataset for repeatable research. Treat unofficial endpoints and wrappers as fragile, verify their terms and output, and never present a failed scrape as “no interest.” BigQuery's public Trends dataset covers top and rising queries, not arbitrary keyword volume.

## Productive-looking failures to prevent

- trend-jacking an unrelated event because it is popular;
- publishing one thin page per Rising query;
- treating a 100 as volume, market share, or conversion intent;
- comparing numbers from different charts without a common configuration;
- calling a low-volume noisy spike a breakout opportunity;
- confusing correlation with campaign lift or causal demand;
- choosing a brand/product name solely from Trends;
- stuffing all variants into titles, headings, alt text, or body copy;
- creating regional doorway pages from map shading;
- copying competitor content or abusing competitor trademarks;
- changing dates without substantive updates;
- reporting only supportive terms while hiding contradictory evidence;
- using short-lived news demand to justify permanent low-value URLs;
- allowing a generative summary in the Trends UI to replace examination of the underlying chart, filters, and exported evidence.

The final recommendation must answer: **Is this a real, brand-relevant user need that we can serve better, and what evidence beyond relative search interest supports the action?**
