# On-Page Writing and QA Thresholds

## Interpret numbers correctly

Google uses rendered width and may rewrite title links or snippets; it publishes no fixed title-character limit and no meta-description length limit. Use these as conservative house checks, not ranking factors or guarantees.

| Element | Preferred | Warning | Hard review gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HTML title | 30–60 characters | 61–72 | Over 72 or duplicate/missing |
| Meta description | 120–160 characters when practical | 161–170 or under 70 | Over 170, duplicate, misleading, or missing on important pages |
| H1 | One clear primary heading | Multiple when semantically justified | Missing, duplicated template text, or inconsistent with purpose |
| URL slug | Short descriptive words | Over ~75 characters | Unstable IDs, tracking data, fragments for primary content, or keyword repetition |
| Image alt | Brief contextual alternative | Long/redundant | Missing on informative images or keyword-stuffed; decorative images should use empty alt |

## Titles

Make every title unique, descriptive, accurate, and aligned with the visible main heading. Put the differentiating topic early; add the brand only when it remains useful and readable. Avoid boilerplate repetition, keyword lists, sensationalism, stale years, fake urgency, and titles that promise content the page does not deliver.

## Meta descriptions

Write a unique one- or two-sentence summary that identifies the page and useful outcome. Include a differentiator or constraint when relevant. Do not stuff keywords, copy a sitewide sentence, fabricate offers, or treat the description as guaranteed snippet text.

## Headings and body

- Use a clear H1 and descriptive nested sections; do not select heading levels for visual size.
- Answer the primary task early, then provide evidence, steps, examples, alternatives, limitations, and next actions.
- Use exact terms naturally and include concepts needed to understand the topic; do not chase keyword density.
- Add original experience, calculations, screenshots, tests, data, or expert review where relevant.
- Cite primary sources close to factual claims and record the date for volatile information.
- Distinguish opinion, inference, sponsored content, and measured fact.
- Avoid arbitrary minimum word counts, filler, repetitive summaries, and AI-style commodity prose.

## Links

Use descriptive anchors that make sense out of context. Link to relevant hubs, prerequisites, deeper evidence, and trustworthy external sources. Fix broken links. Apply `rel="sponsored"` to paid/affiliate placements and `rel="ugc"` to user-generated links; use `nofollow` when association should not be implied.

## Images and video

Use relevant high-quality media near supporting text; stable crawlable URLs; descriptive filenames where practical; explicit dimensions; responsive formats; captions/credits; and accurate alt text. Give important videos dedicated watch pages when appropriate, stable thumbnails, transcripts/captions, and supported metadata.

## Trust and conversion

Make ownership, authorship, contact information, policies, corrections, commercial relationships, and high-stakes expertise clear. Keep calls to action compatible with the informational promise; do not obstruct the main content with intrusive ads/interstitials.

## Content update rule

Update dates only after substantive changes. Record what changed for material revisions. Merge, redirect, archive, or remove content that can no longer be maintained; do not mass-delete solely to make a site appear fresh.
