# Campaign Playbooks

## Product promotion or launch

Audience need → outcome → differentiator → proof/demo → price/availability truth → CTA. Use product/lifestyle imagery, one dominant action, and a real fallback when the hero is blocked. Consider Gmail Promotions cards/deal annotation only when data is accurate.

## Discount promotion

State eligibility, percentage/value, code, start/end with timezone, exclusions, and destination. Never create false scarcity. Keep price and discount math consistent across body, annotation, and landing page. Send reminders based on recipient value, not repeated countdown pressure.

## Newsletter

Open with an editorial promise, curate 1–3 primary stories, add concise summaries, and use hierarchy rather than a wall of cards. Maintain a recognizable cadence and from-name. Offer topic/frequency preferences and a clear unsubscribe.

## Blog/content promotion

Teach one useful idea in the email, then invite the deeper read. Do not send only a headline and button. Mention what is new/original and who benefits. Avoid mailing every post independently when a digest is more respectful.

## Welcome/onboarding

Confirm expectations, deliver the promised resource/value immediately, state frequency, provide one next step, and introduce support/preferences. Sequence by achieved milestone rather than arbitrary time when possible.

## Follow-up/nurture

Add new value each time: answer an objection, provide evidence, show a use case, or clarify the next step. Stop after the defined sequence or on reply/conversion/unsubscribe. Do not send empty “bumping this” messages at scale.

## Cold outreach

Verify jurisdiction, role relevance, list source, identity, legitimate purpose, and opt-out/suppression before drafting. Keep volume low and controlled. Use a real observation, a bounded value hypothesis, and an easy reply. Do not scrape personal addresses, fabricate a referral, or automate a long sequence without response-aware stops.

## Company/product update

Explain what changed, who is affected, when, action required, benefit/tradeoff, documentation, and support. Separate operational notices from promotion. Use version/date and clear status language.

## Order confirmation

Lead with order status/number, merchant identity, items, totals, payment state, address summary, expected next step, support, and secure order link. Avoid promotional clutter. Consider accurate `Order` markup for eligible registered Gmail senders.

## Shipping/delivery update

State current status, carrier, tracking link, destination summary, delivery window, exceptions, and support. Never invent scan events or dates. Consider `ParcelDelivery` and `TrackAction` only when backed by live data.

## Invoice/payment

Show invoice number, amount/currency, due/paid status, date, merchant, secure view/pay action, and support. Avoid attaching executable/unexpected files. Consider Gmail `Invoice` markup where eligible.

## Reservation/event

Provide confirmed status, venue/time/timezone, attendee details, cancellation/change rules, directions/calendar or check-in action, and support. Use the exact supported reservation schema only when registered and truthful.

## Win-back

Use only recipients still eligible to receive marketing. Acknowledge inactivity without surveillance language, offer preference/frequency choices, and sunset persistent non-engagers instead of escalating pressure.

## Plain-text/relationship email

For founder notes and cold outreach, a restrained single-column design or well-formed plain text may outperform a visual campaign. Do not add a hero simply because MJML supports one.
