# Copy and Subject Lines

## Copy architecture

Use: relevance → promise → evidence → action → reassurance. Lead with recipient value, not company autobiography. Keep paragraphs short, front-load meaning, and make the CTA understandable without surrounding copy.

For product promotion: problem/outcome, differentiator, proof/demo, CTA, constraint. For editorial: idea, why it matters, what is inside, read CTA. For transactional: status first, essential details, next action/support, no distracting promotion.

## Subject lines

Write 3–5 variants that test genuinely different angles: outcome, curiosity with clear context, proof, timely utility, or direct offer. Keep them accurate, specific, natural, and recognizable from the sender.

Operational heuristic—not an inbox rule: aim roughly 25–50 characters, place differentiating words early, and test on mobile. Longer subjects are acceptable when clarity requires them. Avoid fake replies/forwards, excessive punctuation/caps, deceptive personalization, unverifiable superlatives, and urgency or scarcity that is not real.

Do not obsess over “spam trigger word” lists. A legitimate discount can say “free shipping”; hiding the offer with awkward synonyms does not repair poor consent or reputation.

## Preview text

Use 40–100 useful characters as a practical starting range. Extend the subject rather than repeat it. State evidence, detail, expiry, or next step. Prevent body/footer text from becoming accidental preview copy. Keep dynamic values safe when missing.

## Personalization

Use a name only when reliable and natural. `Hi {{first_name}},` must have a fallback such as `Hi there,` in the sending/template layer. Prefer behavioral/contextual relevance (“Your saved guide”) over decorative name repetition. Never expose private segments or inferred sensitive traits.

## CTA

Use concrete verbs: “View the collection,” “Read the guide,” “Track order,” “Finish setup,” “Book a demo.” Match the landing page and avoid vague “Click here.” Use one primary CTA, with secondary links only when they serve a distinct task.

## Cold outreach copy

Keep it brief and individually relevant: truthful reason for contact, observed context, credible value hypothesis, low-friction question, real identity, and clear opt-out. Do not pretend familiarity, manufacture compliments, conceal automation, or force a calendar link as the only response.

## Testing

Test one material variable at a time. Define hypothesis, primary metric, guardrails, audience allocation, sample-size caveat, and decision rule before send. Do not declare a winner from tiny or repeatedly peeking samples.
