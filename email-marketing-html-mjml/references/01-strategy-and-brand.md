# Strategy and Brand System

## Campaign brief

Record: message class, audience/list source, recipient stage, objective, single primary action, offer and evidence, sender/reply identity, brand voice, locale, send window, frequency context, required personalization, exclusions, landing page, and success metric.

## Brand extraction

When brand guidelines or a website exist, extract—not guess:

- approved logo variants and safe clear space;
- primary, secondary, background, text, muted, success, warning, and CTA colors;
- accessible color pairs and dark-mode alternatives;
- typography, reliable email fallbacks, capitalization, punctuation, vocabulary, and prohibited phrases;
- photography/illustration style, icon treatment, border/radius style, whitespace density, and CTA voice;
- proof standards, claims restrictions, legal footer, sender identity, and social links.

If a web font is unreliable, use it only as progressive enhancement through `mj-font` and preserve a strong system fallback. Do not force website navigation or every brand color into email.

## Visual direction

Choose a deliberate concept: editorial minimalism, product-led showcase, premium monochrome, bold promotion, warm founder note, data/report, or transactional clarity. Use hierarchy, whitespace, live text, one dominant CTA, restrained accent colors, and purposeful imagery. “Visually stunning” means recognizably branded and easy to act on—not crowded, animation-heavy, or image-only.

Recommended foundation:

- 600px body; 16–24px outer mobile padding;
- 44px minimum touch target where practical;
- body text around 16px with 1.4–1.6 line height;
- one H1-like heading role and logical subsequent levels;
- WCAG AA contrast target; never encode essential information by color alone;
- fallback background colors beneath every image/hero;
- live offer, price, code, and CTA text rather than text baked into images.

## Segmentation

Segment only when it changes relevance: lifecycle stage, purchase category, geography/availability, language, plan, engagement, or expressed preference. Do not use sensitive traits or opaque profiling without a lawful, documented reason. Prefer zero-party and first-party data over inferred attributes.

## Landing-page continuity

Keep message, offer, visual language, price, expiry, and CTA consistent from inbox to landing page. Deep-link to the promised state. Preserve tracking without exposing recipient data. A beautiful email with a mismatched page is a broken campaign.
