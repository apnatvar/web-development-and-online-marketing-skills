# Campaign strategy frameworks

Use frameworks to make a decision, not to decorate a strategy. Select the smallest set that changes the audience, promise, evidence, channel role, offer, or CTA. Record the decision in the strategy; keep framework labels out of audience-facing copy unless they are the subject.

## Evidence level

- **Research-grounded model**: supported by a body of behavioural or marketing research, but still context-dependent.
- **Professional method**: a structured way to organise evidence and choices; useful without claiming scientific prediction.
- **Practitioner heuristic**: a writing or planning aid. Test the result rather than treating the mnemonic as proof.

## Selection map

| Decision problem | Use | Required inputs | Strategy output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience is broad or channels have no distinct role | STP; optionally See–Think–Do–Care | observable segments, needs, behaviours, reachability, objective | selected segment, exclusion, audience state, channel job |
| Claimed need is vague or feature-led | Jobs to Be Done and Value Proposition Canvas | situation, trigger, current workaround, desired progress, pains, gains, evidence | job statement, prioritized pain/gain, capability-to-outcome chain |
| Product, service, price, access, or delivery weakens the promise | 4Ps or 7Ps; reframe with 4Cs or SAVE | product/service, price, distribution, promotion, people, process, evidence | mix inconsistency, corrected offer or explicit constraint |
| Offer blends into competitors | positioning statement, points of parity/difference, reason to believe | category, alternatives, table stakes, differentiator, proof | category frame, one primary value, supported difference |
| Message pillars overlap or lack proof | Message House and benefit ladder | positioning, features, functional/emotional/social benefits, proof | central message, distinct pillars, evidence boundary |
| CTA is too strong for attention or readiness | awareness ladder or See–Think–Do–Care | current knowledge, motivation, perceived risk, next useful action | content role and proportionate CTA |
| Copy needs motivation without pressure | Self-Determination Theory and ELM | user agency, competence barriers, social context, involvement, evidence appetite | autonomy-supportive language, proof depth, friction reduction |
| Choice presentation may distort consent | behavioural-economics audit | options, defaults, comparison, urgency, price framing, cancellation path | disclosed framing, reversible choice, removed dark pattern |
| Campaign needs a balanced publishing system | Hero–Hub–Help and PESO | durable topics, campaign moments, common questions, distribution access | content portfolio and distribution responsibilities |

## Core frameworks

### STP — segmentation, targeting, positioning

**Type:** professional method.

Segment by meaningful differences in situation, need, behaviour, constraints, buying role, or desired outcome. Select a segment because the offer has credible relevance and the channel can reach it, not because it is the largest. Position against the alternative that segment actually uses.

Do not infer needs from demographics alone or collapse users, buyers, approvers, and influencers into one persona.

### Jobs to Be Done and Value Proposition Canvas

**Type:** professional methods.

Express the job as: “When `[situation]`, I want to `[progress]`, so I can `[outcome]`.” Test it against past behaviour, current workarounds, frequency, consequences, and switching barriers. Map only verified jobs, pains, and gains to product capabilities and evidence.

Do not invent customer quotations or turn every feature into a supposed emotional need.

### Marketing-mix audit

**Type:** professional method.

- **4Ps:** Product, Price, Place, Promotion.
- **7Ps for services:** add People, Process, Physical Evidence.
- **4Cs:** Customer, Cost, Convenience, Communication.
- **SAVE:** Solution, Access, Value, Education.

Use one seller-side view and one customer-side view when an attractive campaign cannot repair a weak offer, confusing price, poor access, or untrustworthy service process. Escalate the mismatch instead of writing around it.

### Positioning, parity, difference, and proof

**Type:** professional method.

First establish the points of parity needed to be a credible category member. Then choose one or two points of difference that matter to the target segment. Attach a reason to believe to every material difference. Use perceptual mapping only when based on customer or market evidence; an invented map is not research.

### Motivation and persuasion

- **Self-Determination Theory — research-grounded:** support autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Offer control, explain the path, and avoid belittling the current choice.
- **Elaboration Likelihood Model — research-grounded:** use detailed arguments and evidence when involvement is high; use clear relevance and trustworthy cues when attention is low. A cue must not replace material information.
- **Cialdini principles — research-informed practitioner application:** reciprocity, consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, and unity can suggest tests. Use only genuine proof, limits, and affiliations.

Motivation frameworks explain possible responses; they do not diagnose an individual or guarantee conversion.

### Behavioural-economics audit

**Type:** research-grounded effects with variable strength and context.

Check framing, anchors, reference points, loss language, defaults, social proof, scarcity, and choice overload. Preserve the option set, material terms, total cost, and an easy way to decline or reverse. Never use fake countdowns, hidden defaults, disguised ads, obstructive cancellation, fabricated popularity, selective baselines, or loss framing that exaggerates harm.

### Content portfolio and structure

**Type:** practitioner methods and heuristics.

- **Hero–Hub–Help:** occasional campaign moments, recurring series, and evergreen answers.
- **PESO:** paid, earned, shared, and owned distribution. Do not label exposure as earned unless an independent party chose it.
- **Hook–Value–CTA:** compact educational or promotional asset.
- **Problem–Mistake–Fix / Myth–Reality–Evidence:** corrective education when the evidence supports the correction.
- **Context–Conflict–Resolution / ABT:** narrative compression without manufacturing conflict.

Choose a structure after assigning the asset's job. Do not force every post into a full funnel or repeat the same hook across channels.

## Minimum framework note

In `strategy/campaign-strategy.md`, record:

- decision to make;
- selected framework and why it fits;
- evidence used;
- decision it changed;
- rejected alternative or limitation;
- validation signal or metric.

If the framework changes nothing, omit it.
