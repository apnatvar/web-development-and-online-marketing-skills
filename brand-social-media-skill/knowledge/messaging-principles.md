# Messaging principles

## Central hierarchy

Build every campaign from one hierarchy:

1. Verified product facts and offer terms.
2. Audience problem and desired outcome.
3. Central value proposition.
4. Carefully qualified product promise.
5. Three to five messaging pillars.
6. Proof available for each pillar.
7. Primary and secondary calls to action.

Do not draft channel content until this hierarchy exists in `strategy/`.

## Platform transformation

Preserve product naming, verified facts, positioning, offer, and link destinations. Change the hook, framing, context, tone, length, formatting, CTA strength, link placement, and visual treatment for each channel.

Assign one job to each asset. An educational post may build problem awareness; a case study may handle an objection; an email may ask for a reply. Do not force every asset to explain the whole product.

## Specificity

Prefer an observable problem, mechanism, workflow, tradeoff, or example over a broad adjective. Replace “powerful and innovative” with a supported description of what the product does and for whom.

## Consistency checks

- Use one public product name.
- Use compatible CTA language and identical destinations unless the strategy defines a channel-specific route.
- Trace substantive claims to the evidence inventory.
- Keep unknowns visible.
- Record material assumptions before using them.
