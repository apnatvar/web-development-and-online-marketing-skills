# Why a research decision needs more than a final answer

## The trail scatters before anyone notices

A small product team may capture an interview note in a document, discuss uncertainty in chat, and record only the final choice in a roadmap. Each tool performs its job, but the reasoning behind the decision becomes difficult to revisit.

That matters when the team learns something new, welcomes a new colleague, or needs to understand why an alternative was rejected. The problem is not a missing final answer. It is a missing trail.

## A four-field decision record

Try one lightweight record for each meaningful decision:

- **Source:** the research, observation, or document that informed the decision.
- **Open question:** the uncertainty the team still had.
- **Conclusion:** the choice made at that time.
- **Owner:** the person responsible for revisiting it.

This method can live in an existing document or wiki. Its value is conceptual: keep the evidence and interpretation close to the action they informed.

## The tradeoff

Every record adds maintenance. A team should reserve it for decisions worth revisiting, define who updates it, and archive records that no longer matter. A new tool cannot remove that judgment.
