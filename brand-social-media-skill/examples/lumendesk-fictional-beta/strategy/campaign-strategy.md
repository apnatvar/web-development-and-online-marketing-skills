# Campaign strategy: LumenDesk fictional beta

## Decision summary

- Goal: recruit fictional beta participants while teaching a reusable decision-record method.
- Audience: research leads and product managers in small software teams.
- Primary problem: sources, open questions, and conclusions become separated after a decision.
- Central value proposition: keep the evidence and reasoning for a decision together.
- Product promise: make a fictional decision trail easier to revisit; no performance outcome is claimed.
- Available proof: a fictional product brief and a three-part feature description only.
- Primary CTA: Join the fictional beta.
- Secondary CTA: Read the fictional decision-record field guide.

## Message hierarchy

1. Decision reasoning becomes hard to revisit when its parts live in different tools.
2. A simple record can group the source, open question, conclusion, and owner.
3. Fictional LumenDesk organises work around that decision record.
4. Small research teams can inspect the fictional method and choose whether to join the beta.

## Objections

| Objection | Response | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Another workspace adds process. | Teach the four-field method independently and state that the product is optional. | No efficiency claim. |
| The product may not fit an established stack. | Position the beta as a workflow experiment, not a replacement guarantee. | No integration claims. |
| A fictional beta lacks customer proof. | Say exactly that; demonstrate structure rather than implying adoption. | No customers or results are claimed. |

## Campaign narrative

Begin with the scattered decision trail, teach a small method, show how the fictional product reflects it, then invite qualified teams to examine or test the beta. Community channels lead with the method; owned and outreach channels can make the product relationship more explicit.

## Channel roles

LinkedIn carries professional workflow insight. Facebook hosts an accessible discussion. Reddit contributes the method after community-rule review. Instagram visualises the record. X compresses the observation. Threads opens a conversational question. Medium teaches the full method and tradeoffs. DMs offer the worksheet by permission. Cold email targets a sourced need. The newsletter deepens an existing relationship.

## Measurement suggestions

Track qualified beta requests, field-guide visits, relevant replies, and completion of the fictional beta form. Treat likes, impressions, and editorial scores as context rather than proof of business impact.
