# Direct messages

- Last verified: 2026-07-22
- Official LinkedIn InMail constraint: subject 200 characters; body 1,900 characters.
- Source: [LinkedIn InMail limits](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a411986/inmail-character-limits)
- Unknown constraints: verify the selected LinkedIn, Instagram, X, or community messaging product and account eligibility before sending.

## Purpose and audience behavior

Use DMs to begin a relevant one-to-one conversation, not to deliver a compressed landing page. The recipient should quickly understand who is writing, why this message is relevant, what is known versus inferred, and how to decline.

## Formats

Support LinkedIn, Instagram, and X DMs; community-member, founder-to-founder, prospect, and partnership outreach; and follow-up sequences.

## Sequence

1. Connection or opening: identify a genuine shared context.
2. Context: explain the relevant problem or trigger.
3. Value: offer one useful observation, resource, or product value.
4. Soft CTA: ask a small question or request permission.
5. Follow-up: add value or clarify; do not guilt the recipient.
6. Graceful close: end the sequence and provide an easy decline.

Not every step must be sent. Stop when the recipient declines, does not meet targeting criteria, or the sending policy requires it.

## Personalization

Use fields such as `{{first_name}}`, `{{company}}`, `{{role}}`, `{{observed_problem}}`, `{{relevant_trigger}}`, `{{specific_reference}}`, `{{product_value}}`, and `{{cta}}`. Leave unknowns visible. Never claim to have read, watched, followed, met, or used something unless the source context confirms it.

## Failure modes

Reject fake familiarity, fabricated observations, immediate long pitches, hidden affiliation, mass unsolicited outreach, manipulative follow-ups, false scarcity, and evasion of rate limits or consent controls.

## Validation checklist

- Confirm channel, relationship, source for personalization, and applicable sending rules.
- Keep messages concise and one-purpose.
- Verify product claims and sender affiliation.
- Add an easy decline and a stopping rule.
- Check channel-specific limits before sending.

## Fictional example

> Hi `{{first_name}}` — your public note about preserving research context made me think of a workflow our fictional team is testing. We group each source, open question, and conclusion around one decision in LumenDesk. I built the fictional prototype. Would a one-page version of the method be useful? No worries if not.
