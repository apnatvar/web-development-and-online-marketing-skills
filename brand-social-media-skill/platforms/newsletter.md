# Warm newsletter

- Last verified: 2026-07-22
- Compliance reference: [FTC CAN-SPAM compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- Audience assumption: treat recipients as warm or permission-based only when the user confirms it; verify applicable law and sending-platform rules.

## Purpose and audience behavior

Use newsletters for relationship-based education, product updates, releases, founder context, curated insights, launches, and re-engagement. Subscribers expect value beyond the fact that a product exists and context for why the message belongs in the relationship.

## Formats and package

Support product announcements, educational newsletters, monthly updates, release notes, founder letters, curated digests, launch sequences, and re-engagement. Generate subject options, preview text, opening, body, sections, primary and secondary CTA, plain text, HTML, segmentation notes, optional PS, image guidance, and link map.

## Structure

- Open with a useful insight, industry change, customer problem, practical guide, behind-the-scenes lesson, case evidence, or release context.
- Deliver the promised value before or alongside the product update.
- Use sections and descriptive links for scanning.
- Make the primary action clear; keep the secondary action subordinate.
- Close in the established brand voice.

## Segmentation and re-engagement

State the confirmed relationship and segment. Do not infer engagement or interests absent from data. For re-engagement, explain why the person is receiving the message, offer a preference or exit path, and avoid guilt or false urgency.

## HTML and accessibility

Use simple semantic HTML that remains readable without CSS, descriptive links, alt text, and a content-equivalent plain-text version. Keep approved identity, address, preference, and opt-out content visible.

## Failure modes

Reject cold-prospect language sent to subscribers, product-only filler, clickbait subjects, false personalization, unsupported customer claims, image-only announcements, and a secondary CTA that competes with the campaign goal.

## Validation checklist

- Confirm permission or relationship, segment, and applicable rules.
- Match subject and preview text to the content.
- Provide standalone value and one primary CTA.
- Compare HTML and plain-text meaning.
- Verify links, accessibility, sender identity, and opt-out handling.

## Fictional example

**Subject:** The fictional decision record we now use in retrospectives

**Preview:** A four-field template, what it costs to maintain, and a fictional beta update.

The newsletter first teaches the decision-record method, then explains how the fictional LumenDesk beta supports it, and closes with “Join the fictional beta” plus an approved subscription-management footer.
