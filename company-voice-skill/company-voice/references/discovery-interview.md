# Discovery Interview

Use this workflow when no editorial memory exists or the user asks to define, refine, or audit an organization's voice and content strategy.

## Interview Principles

- Ask in rounds, not as one large questionnaire.
- Start with questions that unlock the most routing decisions.
- Inspect each answer for gaps, contradictions, unsupported claims, and useful specifics.
- Let the user skip, defer, or mark answers provisional.
- Stop when enough reliable information exists to create an editorial foundation.
- Record uncertainty explicitly.

Do not generate posts during initial discovery unless the user explicitly accepts an uncalibrated sample. Even then, label it as exploratory and do not store it as a rule.

## Round 1: Foundation Questions

Ask 5 to 7 questions. Adapt wording to the user and organization.

1. What does the organization do, and for whom?
2. Why does it exist beyond making or selling the product/service?
3. What problem does it solve that its audience already feels?
4. What alternatives does the audience compare it with?
5. How should the organization be perceived after someone reads its content?
6. What should content achieve first: trust, search visibility, leads, comments, clicks, sales enablement, education, hiring, community, or something else?
7. What products, services, pages, resources, repositories, offers, or proof points may content reference?

## Round 2: Differentiation And Authority

Ask only what remains unclear.

- What does the organization believe that competitors understate, miss, or get wrong?
- What topics does it have real authority to discuss?
- Which claims can it support with evidence?
- Which topics, claims, examples, industries, or comparisons should stay outside its content?
- What industry assumptions does it agree with, disagree with, or treat as incomplete?
- What results, clients, credentials, demos, data, repositories, testimonials, or case studies may be referenced?

## Round 3: Voice And Style

Ask targeted questions based on earlier answers.

- How direct or diplomatic should disagreement be?
- How technical should the writing get for each audience?
- How opinionated should the content be?
- How much storytelling, humor, emotion, data, and promotion should appear?
- What styles should it emulate structurally, if any?
- What styles should it reject?
- What words, phrases, tropes, or behaviors should be avoided?
- How should uncertainty be handled?
- How should products be mentioned without becoming over-promotional?

## Round 4: Channels, Formats, And Metrics

Ask this round when content generation or backlog planning is likely.

- Which channels will be used?
- Which formats matter: short social posts, long-form posts, blogs, newsletters, tutorials, case studies, opinion pieces, comparisons, announcements, or reviews?
- Which conversion or engagement actions matter?
- Which success metrics are primary and secondary?
- Is search visibility primary, secondary, or irrelevant?
- Are there publishing constraints, approval rules, legal limits, or cadence expectations?

## Decision States

Use these labels in notes and file metadata:

- `confirmed`: stated or explicitly accepted by the user.
- `inferred`: reasoned from confirmed information; useful but not approved.
- `provisional`: usable for now; expected to change.
- `unresolved`: missing, contradictory, or deferred.
- `retired`: no longer active.

When presenting the foundation, separate confirmed, inferred, provisional, and unresolved decisions.

## Follow-Up Logic

Ask a follow-up when:

- the audience is too broad to choose tone or technical depth
- the positioning lacks a real alternative or contrast
- a desired tone conflicts with credibility rules
- the content goal conflicts with the channel
- a claim lacks evidence
- a product/service reference is unclear
- a search goal is named but no search audience or offer is known
- the user gives adjectives without behavioral examples

Do not ask a follow-up when the missing detail can be safely left unresolved and does not affect the immediate foundation.

## Stop Criteria

Stop discovery when you can draft:

- an organization summary
- a positioning statement
- audience definitions
- desired perception
- voice and tone behavior
- claim/evidence rules
- technical-depth rules
- content goals and metrics
- initial content pillars
- CTA rules
- unresolved questions

Then ask the user to review the foundation before treating inferred or provisional decisions as durable.
