# Strategy Framework Selection

Use this reference during discovery, foundation design, content planning, or review when the editorial memory lacks a defensible decision. A framework is a lens, not a fact source. Select the smallest set that changes a durable rule or a specific content decision.

## Evidence Labels

- **Research-grounded model:** supported by research but context-dependent; do not use it to diagnose individuals or predict guaranteed behaviour.
- **Professional method:** organizes evidence and decisions; quality depends on the inputs.
- **Practitioner heuristic:** helps structure or edit content; it is not evidence of effectiveness.

## Framework Guidance

### Market, customer, and value

**STP — professional method.** Segment by situations, needs, behaviours, constraints, buying roles, or desired outcomes. Target because the organization can credibly serve and reach the segment. Position relative to the alternative that segment uses. Do not infer needs from demographics alone.

**Jobs to Be Done and Value Proposition Canvas — professional methods.** Capture the job as “When `[situation]`, I want to `[progress]`, so I can `[outcome]`.” Validate with past behaviour, current workarounds, frequency, consequences, and switching barriers. Map only verified jobs, pains, and gains to supported capabilities. Do not invent customer language.

**4Ps/7Ps, 4Cs, and SAVE — professional methods.** Use 4Ps (Product, Price, Place, Promotion) or 7Ps for services (add People, Process, Physical Evidence), then check the customer view with 4Cs (Customer, Cost, Convenience, Communication) or SAVE (Solution, Access, Value, Education). Copy cannot repair a weak offer, hidden cost, poor access, or inconsistent service. Store the limitation instead of writing around it.

### Positioning and identity

Establish category **points of parity** before choosing meaningful **points of difference**. Attach a **reason to believe** to every material difference. A positioning statement is an internal scaffold: target, situation, category, distinct value, alternative, differentiator, proof.

Use a **Brand Identity Prism** or **Aaker identity system** only to turn identity into observable behaviour. For example, “competent” should specify evidence standards, explanation depth, and how uncertainty is stated. Archetypes and the Golden Circle are practitioner heuristics; use them for coherence, not as customer truth.

### Motivation, persuasion, and choice

- **Self-Determination Theory — research-grounded:** support autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Avoid controlling language and shame.
- **Elaboration Likelihood Model — research-grounded:** give consequential, high-involvement decisions stronger arguments and evidence. Low attention can justify clarity and credible cues, not omission of material facts.
- **Cialdini principles — research-informed practitioner use:** treat reciprocity, consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, and unity as test ideas. Use only genuine proof, limits, and affiliations.
- **Behavioural-economics effects — research-grounded but variable:** audit anchors, reference points, loss framing, defaults, choice overload, social proof, and scarcity. Preserve informed choice and reversibility.

Never store dark patterns as brand rules: fake urgency, fabricated popularity, misleading comparisons, hidden defaults or costs, obstructive cancellation, guilt, fear without efficacy, or claims that a psychological effect guarantees conversion.

### Content strategy and structures

**Hero–Hub–Help and PESO — practitioner methods.** Balance campaign moments, recurring series, evergreen answers, and paid/earned/shared/owned distribution. Do not call placement “earned” unless an independent party selected it.

**See–Think–Do–Care — professional method.** Match content and CTA to broad interest, active consideration, action, or existing-customer care. Do not treat it as a rigid funnel.

**SCQA, Pyramid Principle, claim–evidence–reasoning, Hook–Value–CTA, Problem–Mistake–Fix, and Context–Conflict–Resolution — practitioner heuristics.** Store them as optional structures with fit and misuse notes. Never manufacture a problem, conflict, open loop, or urgency to satisfy a template.

## Decision Record

For a framework-derived rule, capture:

- decision being made;
- evidence and source status;
- selected framework and why;
- rule or content choice it changed;
- limitation, rejected alternative, or uncertainty;
- how the decision will be reviewed or tested.

Store the resulting decision in the relevant audience, positioning, strategy, voice, rules, content, channel, or metrics file. Do not create a permanent framework file in every editorial memory unless framework provenance is itself useful.
