# Editorial Foundation Workflow

Use this after discovery produces enough information to create or revise the editorial memory.

## Build The Foundation

Create focused files that preserve nuance. Avoid reducing the organization to generic adjectives.

The foundation should cover:

- organization summary
- purpose and problem solved
- positioning and alternatives
- audience definitions
- desired perception
- business and editorial goals
- brand personality
- voice and tone range
- writing principles
- sentence and paragraph behavior
- vocabulary and prohibited language
- evidence and claim rules
- opinion and disagreement rules
- storytelling, humor, emotion, and data usage
- technical-depth rules
- promotional limits and CTA guidance
- credibility mechanisms
- content pillars and recurring themes
- post structures as flexible reasoning frameworks
- search and discoverability goals
- success metrics
- review criteria
- unresolved decisions

## Preserve Decision Status

When writing files:

- Put confirmed decisions in active rule files.
- Label inferred decisions clearly and ask whether to confirm them.
- Put provisional decisions in active files only if they are needed for current work.
- Put unresolved questions in `decisions/unresolved.md`.
- Do not let unresolved questions become generation rules.

## Explain Voice In Practice

For voice and style files, include behavior examples such as:

- how to introduce disagreement
- how to handle uncertainty
- how to criticize an industry trend
- how to explain a technical subject
- how to discuss products without sounding like an ad
- how to use evidence
- how to address non-technical readers
- how to avoid unsupported confidence
- how to reconcile competing arguments

Prefer behavioral rules over adjective lists.

## Content Pillars

Each pillar should define:

- ID
- status
- purpose
- audience relevance
- topics included
- topics excluded
- related beliefs or positioning
- related products/services/resources
- search relevance, if any
- suggested formats
- CTA fit

## Post Structures

Store structures as optional reasoning frameworks. Include when to use and when not to use them.

Example argumentative structure:

1. Introduce the topic.
2. Acknowledge the strongest arguments on both sides.
3. Examine the strongest case in favor.
4. Introduce an overlooked constraint, contradiction, or alternative perspective.
5. Examine the strongest case against.
6. Reconcile the competing positions.
7. Conclude with a relevant CTA.

Also define structures for educational posts, product explanations, technical breakdowns, news summaries, tutorials, case studies, observations, and announcements when needed.

## Foundation Review

Before treating the foundation as durable, show the user:

- confirmed decisions
- inferred decisions needing confirmation
- provisional decisions
- unresolved questions
- content generation risks
- recommended next steps

Ask for corrections or approval of inferred/provisional decisions. Update files accordingly.
